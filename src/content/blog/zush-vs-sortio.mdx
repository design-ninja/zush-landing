Quick answer: [Zush](/ai-file-sorter) and Sortio both sort files into folders from a plain-language prompt and rename them in the same pass. Pick Zush if you sort more than documents and images (video, audio, RAW photos, design files), want exact control over filenames, or prefer a one-time $48 license with no file meter. Pick Sortio if you need Linux, shared team workspaces, or free rule-based sorting.

**Last checked: September 27, 2026.** Written by the maker of Zush. Sortio facts come from [getsortio.com](https://www.getsortio.com/), its pricing and support pages. Verify prices at checkout.

## Where Zush is stronger

**Sorting by content beyond documents.** Zush looks at sampled video frames, audio, design files, and RAW photos, not just text and images. "Group videos by topic" or "Separate B-roll, interviews, and screen recordings" works on files called `IMG_5501.MOV`. Sortio's published materials focus on documents, images, and spreadsheets.

**Filenames you fully control.** In Zush, the folder rule and the filename are separate: Folder rules decide where a file goes, and a Template built from 145+ Naming Blocks decides what it is called, including dates, vendors, amounts, capture metadata, case, separators, and language. That keeps names consistent across thousands of files.

**Price over time.** Zush PRO is $48 once or $10 a month with no monthly file cap, including the managed Zush Cloud AI. Sortio Pro is $99 a year with a monthly AI allowance; BYOK removes that allowance but moves the cost to your provider bill. Over three years, Zush one-time costs $48 and Sortio Pro costs $297.

**Monitor remembers its folders.** When Zush creates a folder for a Monitor Template, it records what belongs there, so next month's invoice from the same supplier lands in the same place.

**Private sorting end to end.** With LM Studio or Ollama selected, both the file analysis and the folder planning step run on your computer, and Zush does not fall back to the cloud.

## Where Sortio is stronger

**Linux.** Sortio runs on Linux as well as Mac and Windows. Zush is Mac and Windows only.

**Free rule-based sorting.** Sortio's free plan includes unlimited classic rules (up to five), watch folders, and undo, without any AI. Zush's free start is 50 AI renames.

**Teams.** Sortio has Team and Enterprise plans with shared workspaces, an admin console, and a central LLM policy. Zush is licensed per user.

**Folder allowlists and fallbacks.** Sortio can restrict sorting to a fixed list of folders and send anything unmatched to a fallback folder. In Zush, Reuse existing folders prefers your folders but can create new ones; add "Put everything else in Other" to your rule for a catch-all.

## Same prompt in both apps

A typical instruction works in either app:

> Group invoices by company and receipts by month. Name month folders YYYY-MM.

In Zush, that sentence goes into **Folder rules**, the Template decides the filename (for example `2026-06-12 – Acme Supply – Invoice 10234.pdf`), and you review both before applying. Sortio builds a plan from the same sentence and routes the files. The difference shows up with mixed batches: add a video, a voice memo, and a Figma export, and check which app gives each one a descriptive name and a sensible folder.

## FAQ

### Is Zush a good Sortio alternative?

Yes. Zush also sorts files into folders from a plain-language prompt and renames them in the same reviewed batch, on Mac and Windows. It adds video, audio, RAW, and design file support, 145+ Naming Blocks for filenames, and a $48 one-time license with no file meter.

### Is Sortio free?

Sortio's free plan includes unlimited rule-based sorting with up to five rules, watch folders, undo, and a one-time AI trial. AI sorting beyond the trial requires Pro at $14.99 a month or $99 a year, or your own API key.

### Which one works offline?

Both. Zush runs analysis and folder planning locally with LM Studio or Ollama. Sortio supports Ollama for local AI.

### Does Zush run on Linux?

No. Zush has native apps for macOS 15+ and Windows 10 and 11. If you need Linux, Sortio or the open-source AI File Sorter are the options.
