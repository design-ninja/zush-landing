Quick answer: [Zush](/ai-file-sorter) and Sortio both sort files into folders from a plain-language prompt and rename them in the same pass. Pick Zush if you sort more than documents and images (video, audio, RAW photos, design files), want exact control over filenames, want to start without an account, or prefer a <BlogHighlight>$48 one-time license that includes managed cloud AI</BlogHighlight>. Pick Sortio if you need Linux or shared team workspaces with an admin console.

## Where Zush is stronger

**Sorting by content beyond documents.** Zush names and sorts videos from speech, embedded subtitles, and metadata (including MKV), plus audio, design files, and RAW photos. "Separate B-roll, interviews, and screen recordings" works on files called `IMG_5501.MOV`. Sortio added photo and video location metadata in v8.66.0, so it can group clips by place, but its published materials still focus on documents, images, and spreadsheets.

**Filenames you fully control.** In Zush, the folder rule and the filename are separate: Folder rules decide where a file goes, and a Template built from 145+ Naming Blocks decides what it is called, including dates, vendors, amounts, capture metadata, sequence numbers, case, separators, and language. You preview every name, can double-click one to edit it, and nothing moves until you click Rename.

**Works right away.** Zush's first 50 AI renames run on managed Zush Cloud AI with no account, card, API key, or model download. Sortio asks for an email account before you start. In cloud mode Zush needs 4 GB of RAM and about 200 MB of disk; Sortio's local models need at least 8 GB of RAM and 10 GB of disk.

**A one-time license that includes the cloud.** Zush PRO is $48 once or $10/month with no rename cap, and the one-time license includes Zush Cloud AI, BYOK, Ollama, LM Studio, all future updates, and up to 3 devices across Mac and Windows. Sortio Pro is a subscription with a hosted AI allowance that its pricing page doesn't publish as a fixed number of files; BYOK is unmetered but provider charges are separate. A three-year license-only scenario is $48 for Zush One-Time and $297 for Sortio Pro Annual, assuming unchanged prices, before taxes or API costs. See [Zush pricing](/pricing).

**Monitor remembers its folders.** When Zush creates a folder for a Monitor Template, it records what belongs there, so next month's invoice from the same supplier lands in the same place. On Mac, Monitor also watches external drives and resumes after you reconnect them.

**Private sorting end to end.** With LM Studio or Ollama selected, both the file analysis and the folder planning step run on your computer, and Zush does not fall back to the cloud.

Zush also supports nested folder rules, such as grouping by year and then month, and can sort already renamed files without another rename credit. You can [export Templates](/docs/template-import-export) with Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, and folder rules to another computer. [AI folder sorting](/docs/folder-sorting) works on Mac and Windows.

## Where Sortio is stronger

**Linux.** Sortio runs on Linux as well as Mac and Windows. Zush is Mac and Windows only.

**Teams.** Sortio's Team plan includes shared workspaces and automations, an admin console, a central LLM policy, and a pooled allowance at twice Pro per seat. Enterprise starts at $50/seat/month with volume licensing for 25+ seats, audit logs, and procurement support. Zush's per-user licensing has no shared admin workspace.

**Free rule-based sorting.** Sortio's free plan includes rule-based sorting without AI (up to five rules and three Spaces), preview, and undo. Local models via Ollama or LM Studio and your own API key also work on the free plan. Watch folders are no longer part of it.

**Folder allowlists and fallbacks.** Sortio can restrict sorting to a fixed list of folders and send anything unmatched to a fallback folder. In Zush, Reuse existing folders prefers your folders but can create new ones; add "Put everything else in Other" to your rule for a catch-all.

## Sortio limitations to check before buying

**Hosted retention and remote metadata.** Sortio's homepage says hosted content is purged on a 30-day window. By default, AI planning sends filenames and metadata; content-based features can also send file text or images. For a strict no-upload requirement, select a local model (Ollama or LM Studio) and verify it. BYOK sends data to your selected provider; it is not automatically offline.

**Account and allowance.** Sortio needs an email account even for the free plan. Pro and Team are recurring subscriptions with hosted allowances shown as a percentage indicator rather than a fixed count; Pro AI sorts are listed at up to 5,000 files per run. The App Store lists Pro at $19.99/month versus $14.99 on the website, so check where you buy.

**Backup size.** Sortio keeps backups for the ten most recent sorts and skips a backup when the batch exceeds its default 500 MB cap. You can change or remove that cap; history-based undo is separate. For a large media batch, check the backup setting rather than assuming a safety copy exists.

These limits don't erase its Linux and team strengths. Zush's own constraints are no Linux build and no shared team workspace.

## Same prompt in both apps

A typical instruction works in either app:

> Group invoices by company and receipts by month. Name month folders YYYY-MM.

In Zush, that sentence goes into **Folder rules**, the Template decides the filename (for example `2026-06-12 – Acme Supply – Invoice 10234.pdf`), and you review both before applying. Sortio builds a plan from the same sentence and routes the files. The difference shows up with mixed batches: add a video, a voice memo, and a Figma export, and check which app gives each one a descriptive name and a sensible folder.

## FAQ

### Is Zush a good Sortio alternative?

Yes, on Mac and Windows. Zush also sorts files into folders from a plain-language prompt and renames them in the same reviewed batch. It adds video named from speech and subtitles, audio, RAW, and design file support, 145+ Naming Blocks for filenames, and persistent batch undo. You can start without an account. If you need Linux or a team admin console, Sortio remains the better fit.

### Is Sortio free?

Partly. Sortio's free plan includes rule-based sorting with up to five rules and three Spaces, preview, undo, and a one-time AI trial, and it needs an email account. Watch folders require Pro at $14.99 a month or $99 a year on the website. Ongoing AI sorting needs Pro, your own API key, or a local model via Ollama or LM Studio, which Sortio allows on the free plan too. Zush's free tier gives 50 AI renames with no account, card, or API key.

### Which is better value: Zush $48 or Sortio Pro?

Over three years, Zush One-Time costs $48 and Sortio Pro Annual costs $297 at current prices, before taxes or API costs. Zush's license includes managed Zush Cloud AI with no rename cap, BYOK, local models, all future updates, and up to 3 devices. Sortio Pro adds a hosted AI allowance and watch folders; BYOK, local models and Linux are available on every Sortio plan. For teams that need shared workspaces, Sortio's Team plan has no Zush equivalent.

### Which one works offline?

Both. Zush runs file analysis and folder planning locally with LM Studio or Ollama and does not fall back to the cloud. Sortio supports local models via Ollama or LM Studio on every plan, including Free, with a stated minimum of 8 GB of RAM (16 GB or more recommended) and 10 GB of disk. In hosted mode, Sortio sends filenames and metadata by default and says hosted content is purged on a 30-day window.

### Does Zush run on Linux?

No. Zush has native apps for macOS 15 or later on Apple Silicon and Intel, and for Windows 10 and 11. If you need Linux, Sortio is one option, and the open-source AI File Sorter is another. Zush's files stay on a Mac or PC, so a Linux-only setup is a real gap.
