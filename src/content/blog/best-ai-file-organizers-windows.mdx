## Rename in place or move into folders?

Every AI file organizer does one or both of these:

1. It renames files so File Explorer and Windows Search can find them by what they contain.
2. It moves files into a folder structure, sometimes one it invents.

Both change file paths, so shortcuts and project links can break. Moving is more disruptive, which is why it needs a preview and an undo that survives a restart. If you open files just to find out what they are, you have a naming problem first.

## 1. Zush: content-aware names and AI folder sorting

[Zush for Windows](/windows) runs on Windows 10 and 11 and installs from the [Microsoft Store](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9ND4WVZSDQ3X). Drop in a folder of screenshots, PDFs, scans, photos, Office documents, design files, audio or video. Zush reads each file and suggests a name like `2026-09-18_acme_invoice_1042.pdf` or `stripe-revenue-dashboard-september.png`. Video is named from speech, embedded subtitles and metadata, MKV included, and music can be identified even without tags.

It works right away. The first 50 AI renames run on managed Zush Cloud AI with no account, card, API key or model download, and cloud mode needs only 4 GB of RAM. When folders should never leave the PC, switch to Ollama or LM Studio; optional on-device speech recognition keeps video transcription local too.

You see every suggestion before anything changes. Double-click a name to edit it, regenerate one, or apply the batch. Ready-made Templates (including Movies & TV and Remove Duplicates) and 145+ Naming Blocks keep names consistent: dates, vendor, amount, client, sequence numbers with leading zeros.

To file things too, use Auto or write a rule such as “Group invoices by company and receipts by month.” Preview the groups, choose a destination, and reuse existing folders. Rules can be nested and can use file details such as dates and page counts, and you can sort files while keeping their current names. AI Rename also detects duplicates: keep them, mark them in the name, or move them to the Recycle Bin. Copies use no credits, and Undo restores them. Step-by-step: [sort files into folders with AI on Windows](/blog/sort-files-into-folders-with-ai-windows) and [30 ready-made folder rules](/blog/ai-folder-sorting-prompts).

## 2. Sortio: prompt-based sorting, plus Linux

Sortio takes a plain-language instruction ("put invoices by year, screenshots by project"), shows a plan, then renames, creates folders and moves files. It runs on Windows 10/11, macOS and Linux, and you sign in with an email account.

The free plan covers rule-based sorting with up to five rules and three Spaces, a one-time AI trial, preview, sort history and undo. Watch folders and automations now need Pro, at $14.99 per month or $99 per year with a monthly AI allowance. Your own key bypasses that allowance, and Ollama keeps processing local (8 GB RAM minimum).

Choose Sortio if you need Linux or team seats. Weakness: hosted AI is a monthly allowance, and the free plan no longer watches folders. See [Zush vs Sortio](/blog/zush-vs-sortio).

## 3. Wisfile: free option for documents

Wisfile is free and runs entirely on your device, on Windows and Mac. It renames documents from their content (title, author, year) and can group a batch into topic folders. Templates such as `Author-Year-Title` are supported.

![Wisfile's own illustration of random PDF names turned into dated, descriptive document filenames](/images/blog/competitors/wisfile.webp)

Choose Wisfile for a one-off cleanup of a PDF and Word archive when budget matters most. Weakness: it has no undo and no folder monitoring, and it focuses on PDF and Word rather than images or video. Work on copies.

## 4. RenameClick: a bundled local model

RenameClick's free AI path is a built-in local model, about a 4 GB download with 16 GB of RAM recommended. You do not need to set up Ollama, which is a real convenience. It renames images, documents, audio and, since version 4.1.2, video (MP4, MOV, M4V and 3GP, using frames plus local speech transcription). MKV, WebM, AVI, RAW photos and design files are not in its format list. Auto Flow renames and moves new files while the app runs.

The free tier gives unlimited analysis and 100 renames or moves a month; the app must go online once before the first apply. Lifetime is $36 plus VAT for up to 3 devices on direct builds, and refunds are offered only where the law requires. Standard Rename keeps 10 undo entries in memory, cleared on restart. Ratings: 3.0 from 6 on the Microsoft Store (US), 3.5 from Softpedia's editors.

![RenameClick (Mac version shown) sorting renamed files into Legal Documents, Medical Documents, and Uncategorized before Rename & Move](/images/blog/competitors/renameclick.webp)

Choose RenameClick if you want offline AI with no configuration and enough RAM. Full breakdown: [Zush vs RenameClick](/blog/zush-vs-renameclick).

## 5. NudgeFile: free local-AI folder watcher

NudgeFile is a newer Windows 10 and 11 app, a free download from the Microsoft Store. Its site says it monitors folders such as Downloads and Desktop, gives new files standardized names, routes them to destination folders, and finds duplicates with checksums plus local visual AI. AI runs locally through Ollama, with a cloud option. A "Human Approval Pipeline" lets you review suggestions or let them run automatically, and the site says any action can be undone with one click.

Choose NudgeFile if you want a free, always-on local organizer on Windows. Weakness: its FAQ says the model analyzes "file metadata and structure", and the site lists neither supported file types nor any paid tiers, so test it on your own files first.

## 6. Foldora: one-off local cleanup for $14.99

Foldora runs on Windows 10, 11 and macOS. It analyzes selected files locally, suggests folders and clearer filenames, and applies them after you approve the preview. It costs $14.99 lifetime. Purchase, activation and periodic license checks need an internet connection.

Choose Foldora for a cheap, local, one-time tidy of a mixed batch. Weakness: it has no folder monitoring, and the vendor itself says a guaranteed one-click undo is not advertised, so keep a backup before large changes.

## 7. File Juggler: predictable rules without AI

File Juggler is a long-running Windows automation tool, still maintained (version 3.1.9 shipped October 30, 2026). You write rules ("if a PDF in Downloads contains 'Invoice', rename it with the date and move it to Finance"), and it applies them as files arrive. It can read PDF, Word and Excel text for those rules. Softpedia's editors rate it 4.5/5.

It costs $50 plus tax as a one-time license with a full-featured 30-day trial. Weakness: there is no AI and no OCR, so every case needs a rule, and image-only scans need a separate OCR step. More options: [File Juggler alternatives](/blog/file-juggler-alternatives).

## 8. DropIt: free rules engine

DropIt is free and open source. Rules match files by name, size, date or regex; you drag files onto it or let it scan monitored folders on a timer. It has 21 actions, including move, copy, rename, compress and extract.

Weakness: the latest release, 8.5.1, is from February 2018, so expect no updates or AI. Choose DropIt for simple free sorting by extension or name pattern. If you came from a Mac, see [Hazel for Windows alternatives](/blog/hazel-for-windows-alternatives).

## Test any organizer on 10 files first

Copy ten typical files into a test folder: a screenshot, an invoice, a receipt, a phone photo, a spreadsheet, a Word document, a slide deck, a video, an audio file, and a random download. Then check:

1. Are the results specific enough to search for?
2. Could you see every change before it happened?
3. Can you undo the whole batch, even after restarting the app?
4. Does the price still work at your real monthly volume?

For a deeper checklist, read [how to safely batch rename files](/blog/how-to-safely-batch-rename-files).

## FAQ

### What is the best AI file organizer for Windows 11?

Zush is our pick: it renames files by content and sorts them into folders using Auto or your own instructions, with a preview, Templates, monitoring and persistent undo. Sortio is the alternative if you need Linux, RenameClick if you want a bundled local model, and File Juggler if exact rules without AI fit your files. We make Zush, so test any of them on copies first.

### Is there a free AI file organizer for Windows?

Yes. Wisfile is free and local for PDF and Word files, and NudgeFile is a free Microsoft Store download. Zush gives 50 free AI renames with no account, card or API key. RenameClick allows 100 renames or moves a month after a 4 GB model download. DropIt is free too, but it uses fixed rules rather than AI.

### Which Windows organizer gives the most for a one-time price?

Foldora costs $14.99 and RenameClick $36 plus VAT, but both run on local or your own AI only. Zush's $48 license includes the managed Zush Cloud AI plus BYOK, Ollama and LM Studio, unlimited renames, all future updates, up to 3 devices across Windows and Mac, and a 14-day refund. File Juggler's $50 license buys rules, not AI.

### Does an AI file organizer upload my files?

It depends on the mode. Zush with Ollama or LM Studio keeps analysis on your PC; Zush Cloud AI and BYOK send file content to a cloud model for naming. Wisfile, Foldora and RenameClick's built-in model work locally, and NudgeFile defaults to local AI. Sortio offers hosted AI, plus BYOK or Ollama and LM Studio on every plan. Check each app's AI setting before pointing it at sensitive folders.

### Can AI organize my Downloads folder automatically on Windows?

Yes. Zush can monitor Downloads, rename new files and apply the folder rules saved in your Template. RenameClick's Auto Flow, Sortio Pro and NudgeFile also watch folders. Start with one test folder before you automate important directories. The [Downloads automation guide](/ai-file-sorter) walks through the Zush setup.
