## What each one is actually for

**The Terminal** is the honest baseline, and most people skip past it too fast. Three lines list every exact duplicate in a folder and change nothing:

```bash
find ~/Downloads -type f -print0 | xargs -0 shasum -a 256 | sort > /tmp/hashes.txt
awk '{print $1}' /tmp/hashes.txt | uniq -d > /tmp/dupes.txt
grep -F -f /tmp/dupes.txt /tmp/hashes.txt
```

It is free, it cannot delete anything by accident, and for one folder it is faster than installing an app. What it does not give you is a review interface, which is the thing you are paying for everywhere below.

**The Photos Duplicates collection** is the right tool whenever the duplicates are inside your photo library. In macOS 26 it sits under **Library** in the Photos sidebar. It merges rather than deletes, keeping the higher quality version along with keywords, favorites, and album membership. It covers only the library, so photos sitting loose in a folder are invisible to it. For choosing a whole photo library app, see [the best photo organizers for Mac](/blog/best-photo-organizing-software-mac).

**dupeGuru** is free, open source, and the most flexible of the apps. It has separate modes for general files, music, and pictures, and the picture mode does fuzzy matching with an adjustable threshold. Its results pane lets you mark files and then send them to the Trash, move them, or copy them to another folder. Rename Selected renames one selected file at a time, so it is a manual fix, not a bulk rename. The interface shows its age and its defaults expect you to understand what a match threshold does.

**Duplicate File Finder from Nektony** has a free version and a Pro upgrade from $7.95 per month. Nektony's page says Pro adds "additional features and advanced options" without listing which ones, so test the free version on your folder before paying. It also finds similar folders, which is the feature you want after a sync went wrong and the same directory exists twice under different names.

**Gemini 2** is the polished one. Smart Select proposes what to remove and learns from what you keep and delete, which helps on a library of tens of thousands of photos and is risky on a folder of work documents. It finds similar images, not only exact copies, and its Duplicates Monitor notifies you about new copies. It costs $1.95 per month or $44.95 one-time, and the trial cleans up to 500 MB before you pay.

**CleanMyMac** includes duplicate and similar-photo finding inside a general cleanup suite. If you already own it, use what you have. Buying it for duplicates alone means paying for cache cleaning and uninstallers you did not ask for.

**Zush** is an AI file renamer with duplicate detection built in, not a disk-wide duplicate scanner. It compares the files in the batch you give it, matches exact copies by content, and handles them inside the renaming pass. It does not find similar photos or scan a whole disk.

## Where Zush fits

The case for a separate duplicate finder is strongest when cleaning is the whole job: a photo library, a backup drive, a folder that only needs to get smaller.

The case against it is the folder that has two problems at once. A Downloads folder full of `scan_0041 (1).pdf` and `IMG_2291 copy.png` needs the copies gone *and* the survivors named, and a duplicate finder leaves you halfway. You run one app, then open another, and the second one re-reads files the first one already read.

In Zush the detection is a Template setting called **Detect duplicate files**. Copies are matched by content before analysis and never sent to the AI, so they use no credits, and each one is labelled in the list with the file it matches. Nothing changes until you click Rename.

![Zush showing detected copies in the AI Rename list, each labelled with the file it duplicates](/images/showcase/macos/duplicates-keep-light.webp)

Then they go one of three ways. Keep as is leaves them untouched and unchecked. Mark in name gives each copy the original's new name with a Duplicate Mark appended, so copies sort next to their originals. Move to Trash removes them when you apply the batch, with the count shown next to the Rename button.

![Zush with Move to Trash selected, showing a Moves to Trash label on each copy and the Copies to Trash counter](/images/showcase/macos/duplicates-trash-light.webp)

The difference is what happens afterwards. Removals are recorded in Activity alongside the renames, and Undo restores the copies to their original folders under their original names and reverses the renames in the same action, as long as the Trash has not been emptied. The other apps send files to the Trash too, so recovery is possible there, but you restore them from the Trash yourself.

There is also a Remove Duplicates Template, built from a single Original Name block, for when the names are already right and you only want the copies cleared. Duplicate detection arrived in Zush 3.23 for Mac and 3.19 for Windows, and the [duplicate files guide](/docs/duplicates) covers every option.

## What none of them do

Exact matching will not pair two scans of the same page, a photo and its edited export, or the same document saved as `.docx` and `.pdf`. Similar-image matching handles the photo case, with a judgement call on every pair. The others need you.

And no duplicate finder protects you from a bad decision at speed. Whatever you use, send files to the Trash rather than deleting them, leave the Trash unemptied for a few days, and be suspicious of any feature that selects what to delete on your behalf without showing its reasoning.

## FAQ

### Does macOS have a built-in duplicate file finder?

Only for the Photos library and for Music. In macOS 26, Photos shows a Duplicates collection under Library that merges matching photos and videos. Music can show duplicate songs. Finder has no general duplicate finder for documents and downloads, which is why people use the Terminal command above, a free app like dupeGuru, or a paid one like Gemini 2.

### Is a free duplicate finder good enough?

For exact copies, yes. dupeGuru is free and open source, and the Terminal command lists exact copies without deleting anything. Nektony's Duplicate File Finder has a free version, though the vendor does not say exactly what Pro adds. Paying, for example $44.95 one-time for Gemini 2, buys a smoother review screen, similar-photo matching, and automatic selection on large libraries.

### What is the safest way to run any of these?

Scan one folder rather than a whole disk, read the groups before acting, delete to the Trash, and leave the Trash alone for a few days. Avoid auto-selection on folders of work documents, where the rule picking the keeper is not one you wrote. Tools that show every removal in a preview and offer an Undo make a mistake cheaper to fix.

### Can a duplicate finder also rename my files?

dupeGuru has Rename Selected, which renames one selected file at a time by hand. Zush works the other way round: it is an AI renamer that names files from their contents and handles exact copies in the same pass, keeping them, marking them in the name, or moving them to the Trash. The other apps in this comparison remove or move duplicates but do not rename.

### Do duplicates cost extra to process in Zush?

No. Copies are identified by content before analysis and never sent to the AI, so a folder that is half duplicates costs the same as the same folder with one of each. The free tier gives 50 AI renames with no account or card, and PRO is $10 per month or $48 one-time for unlimited renames on up to 3 Mac or Windows devices.

### Do these apps upload my files to scan them?

dupeGuru, the Terminal command, and Apple Photos compare files on your Mac. Gemini 2, CleanMyMac, and Duplicate File Finder are Mac apps that scan the folders you choose; check each vendor's privacy policy if that matters. Zush detects duplicates locally by comparing file contents, and copies are never sent to the AI. The originals it renames go to Zush Cloud AI by default, or stay on your Mac when you choose Ollama or LM Studio.
