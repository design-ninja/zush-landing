### 2. FilesDesk

FilesDesk is included because it targets a similar Mac + Windows workflow with managed AI, BYOK/local modes, Watch Folders, templates, batch processing, and Processing History. Its main limitation is structural: managed suggestions are metered, while the lifetime path requires your own API key or local model. For the direct trade-offs, see [Zush vs FilesDesk](/blog/zush-vs-filesdesk).

### 3. RenameClick

RenameClick is included for its AI Search and rename-plus-folder-routing workflow. It ships desktop apps for macOS and Windows with built-in local AI, Ollama, LM Studio, optional cloud providers, and Auto Flow. Local processing is not treated as an advantage because several products here, including Zush, support it. Its published coverage excludes camera RAW, design, and video files. Its [Rename workspace documentation](https://rename.click/docs/getting-started/how-it-works) limits Undo to 10 entries in memory, cleared on restart; this is not persistent history.

### 4. AI Renamer

Two separate projects share this name, and conflating them overstates what the paid product offers. **AI Renamer at airenamer.app** is a commercial Mac and Windows desktop app whose pricing page lists exactly three things: Local Mode at $19 one-time, Cloud Mode at $10 per 200 credits, and 10 free credits on signup. It advertises no CLI and no open-source edition. **`ozgrozer/ai-renamer`** is an unrelated free GPL-3.0 Node.js CLI on GitHub that runs on Ollama, LM Studio, or an OpenAI key and handles images and videos (via ffmpeg). When you see "AI Renamer is free and open source," that is the GitHub project, not the $19 app.

### 5. Renamer.ai

Renamer.ai documents desktop and web workflows, Magic Folders, templates, preview, and undo. Paid plans are metered, and a user-configurable BYOK, Ollama, or LM Studio connection is not documented.

Its [Windows page](https://renamer.ai/ai-file-renamer-windows) describes on-device processing, while its [privacy policy](https://renamer.ai/privacy-policy/) says file contents are transmitted to AI subprocessors. These statements do not establish a consistent offline data flow. We have not independently tested its network behavior. See [Zush vs Renamer.ai](/blog/zush-vs-renamer-ai) for the separate pricing comparison.

### 6. NameQuick

NameQuick now publishes Windows and Mac downloads. Its documented Mac workflow covers Finder integration, Watch Folders, Rules, undo, OCR, and 30+ format categories. The vendor does not publish a complete platform-by-platform matrix, so this comparison does not assume every Mac-specific feature is present on Windows.

### 7. Wisfile

Wisfile markets local file renaming without fees. The checked public materials do not provide a complete platform-and-format matrix or a documented persistent recovery workflow; its marketing claims were not independently tested.

### 8. Riffo

Riffo is a Mac app and web demo for AI-powered file renaming and folder organization. Its public homepage positions the product as free and shows bulk file rename plus categorizing files into folders. Beyond that, there is very little to verify — no pricing page, no format list, no undo statement, and no folder-monitoring statement on the pages checked.

### 9. Renamed.to

Renamed.to documents cloud-storage integrations, an API, and document review controls. Its workflow is cloud-based and metered, rather than a native desktop workflow with local AI and persistent desktop Rename History. Connected storage does not by itself establish that file content never leaves that storage.

### 10. File Renamer AI

File Renamer AI appears in current search results, so it is included for market coverage, not as a general recommendation. Its published workflow uploads files in a browser, processes up to 100 images per batch, and returns renamed files in a ZIP. The vendor FAQ lists JPG, JPEG, PNG, WEBP, GIF, and other common images with a 10 MB limit per file.

The published browser workflow is image-focused, with a 10 MB file cap and 100-image batch cap. Persistent rollback and watched-folder automation are not documented. These are workflow boundaries, not measured accuracy scores.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which app is strongest for mixed Mac and Windows folders?

For mixed Mac and Windows folders, Zush is the strongest recommendation whether the batch contains common images and documents or expands into RAW photos, design files, iWork, video, audio, screenshots, and PDFs.

### How do I use Zush offline?

Connect LM Studio or Ollama to a compatible vision-capable model on your computer. Test the local connection, review a small batch, and use Rename History after applying. Both modes share Zush's Templates and preview controls. Follow the [offline setup](/offline-ai-file-renamer) for requirements and limits.

### What does BYOK mean in Zush?

BYOK means bring your own key: connect Gemini, OpenRouter, OpenAI, or Claude through your own provider account. Provider usage is billed separately. LM Studio and Ollama are separate local modes; they are not cloud BYOK providers.

### Is an AI file renamer also a file organizer?

Zush is both a content-aware renamer and a naming-first organizer: it combines folder monitoring, Templates, Naming Blocks, Finder tags, metadata, preview, and undo. Some competitors emphasize movement, search, or document-only automation, but those narrower scopes do not displace Zush as the overall recommendation.

### Is it safe to rename files in bulk with AI?

It is safer when the tool provides preview and rollback. Bulk AI renaming should never apply hundreds of filenames blindly. Use a tool that shows every proposed name, handles duplicates, preserves extensions, and keeps rename history so a bad batch can be reverted.
