## How AI file sorters decide where a file goes

Every sorter needs a way to pick the folder. There are three:

| Approach | You provide | Example | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prompt** | A sentence describing the folders | "Group invoices by company and receipts by month" | Zush, Sortio |
| **Category list** | Folder names the AI picks from | Invoices, Receipts, Contracts, Other | RenameClick, AI File Sorter |
| **Conditions** | If/then rules on name, date or text | If name contains "invoice", move to Finance | Hazel, NameQuick Rules, File Juggler, DropIt |

A prompt is the most flexible because folders can be created from what the files contain: one per supplier, client, month or subject. A category list is predictable but you maintain it. Conditions are exact but miss any file that does not match the pattern. Sparkle builds its own library with little input from you.

## 1. Zush: best overall AI file sorter

Zush does two jobs in one reviewed batch: it names each file by its content and sorts it into a folder. You decide how the folders work in **Folder rules**, a plain-language field that accepts up to 2,000 characters. Leave it empty and Auto groups files by content into a few broad, reusable folders.

![Zush Folder rules dialog with the instruction to group invoices by company and receipts by month, plus example rules](/images/showcase/macos/folder-rules-light.webp)

What sets it apart:

- **It reads more kinds of files.** PDFs and image-only scans, photos including RAW and HEIC, screenshots, Office and iWork documents, design files, audio, and video including MP4, MOV, MKV, AVI and WMV. Video is understood from frames, speech, embedded subtitles and metadata, so "Group videos by topic" works.
- **Names and folders are planned together.** Your Template sets the filename, Folder rules set the location, and you see both before anything moves.
- **It files into folders you already have.** With Reuse existing folders on, Zush matches files to suitable folders and creates a new one only when nothing fits. Nested rules (year, then month) work too.
- **It catches duplicates.** AI Rename can keep, mark or trash copies; copies use no credits, and Undo restores them.
- **It keeps sorting new files.** Assign the Template to Monitor and new downloads or scans are renamed and sorted as they arrive.
- **It starts free and stays one-time.** 50 free AI renames on Zush Cloud AI with no account, card or key. PRO is $10 a month or $48 one-time, and the one-time license covers unlimited renames in every mode, cloud included, on up to 3 devices across Mac and Windows.

With LM Studio or Ollama, both analysis and folder planning stay on your computer. Rename History persists after a restart, and Undo All restores a whole batch and flags conflicts. You can also sort files without renaming them, on Mac and Windows, and use rules based on file details such as dates and page counts (the Mac app also uses size, camera and duration). Setup guides: [Mac](/blog/sort-files-into-folders-with-ai-mac) and [Windows](/blog/sort-files-into-folders-with-ai-windows).

## 2. Sortio: closest alternative, plus Linux

Sortio is also prompt-driven: describe the organization in plain English, review the plan, and it renames files, creates folders and routes them. It can restrict sorting to existing folders and send unmatched files to a fallback. It runs on macOS, Windows and Linux, and recent versions read photo and video location metadata.

The free plan has unlimited rule-based sorting with up to five rules and three Spaces, a one-time AI trial, preview, sort history and undo. Watch folders and automations need Pro: $14.99 per month or $99 per year with a monthly AI allowance (the App Store lists $19.99/month). Local models via Ollama or LM Studio and BYOK are available on every plan, including Free. You sign in with an email account.

Choose Sortio if you need Linux or team seats. Weakness: the free plan no longer watches folders, and hosted AI use is metered unless you bring a key or run a local model. Full breakdown: [Zush vs Sortio](/blog/zush-vs-sortio).

## 3. RenameClick: sort into categories you define

RenameClick has three sorting methods: **folder sets**, where the AI picks from a built-in or custom set; **Metadata Folders** built from dates or EXIF fields; and **Match Folders**, which reads the existing subfolders of your output folder up to three levels deep. Auto Flow watches a folder and moves or copies files while the app runs.

![RenameClick (Mac version shown) sorting renamed files into Legal Documents, Medical Documents, and Uncategorized before Rename & Move](/images/blog/competitors/renameclick.webp)

Its free AI path is a bundled local model (about 4 GB, 16 GB of RAM recommended), so you skip Ollama setup. Since version 4.1.2 it handles MP4, MOV, M4V and 3GP video; MKV, WebM, AVI, RAW and design files are not supported. The free plan includes 100 renames or moves a month, and lifetime is $36 plus VAT.

Choose RenameClick if your categories are fixed and known in advance. Weakness: rename undo keeps 10 entries in memory and clears on restart. See [Zush vs RenameClick](/blog/zush-vs-renameclick).

## 4. Sparkle: hands-off cleanup for a Mac

Sparkle organizes Desktop, Downloads, Documents and other folders on a schedule. It builds an AI Library of folders from your files, flags duplicates, and can revert its organization.

It is Mac only, has no free tier (the 15-day trial needs a card), and costs $119 a year for one Mac or $179 lifetime for up to five. It does not rename files.

Choose Sparkle for set-and-forget cleanup of a personal Mac. Weakness: you do not spell out the folder logic, and filenames stay as they were. See [Sparkle alternatives](/blog/sparkle-alternatives).

## 5. NameQuick: rules after renaming

NameQuick renames documents by content and then runs conditional Rules that move a file into a folder, add tags, set a color label or archive it. It runs on Mac and Windows.

![NameQuick promo image showing scanned invoices, receipts, and contracts renamed with dates and vendor names](/images/blog/competitors/namequick.webp)

You get 30 free Managed renames with no card. Managed plans start at $12 a month for 500 renames, rename packs cost $19 for 500 or $39 for 1,500, and the $69 Self-Managed license needs your own API key or a local model.

Choose NameQuick for if/then routing after AI naming. Weakness: each destination needs its own rule. See [Zush vs NameQuick](/blog/zush-vs-namequick).

## 6. AI File Sorter: free and open source

AI File Sorter is a free, AGPL-3.0 desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux (version 1.9.2). It suggests categories and optional subcategories for each file, supports whitelists of allowed categories, reads images with a local vision model, extracts text from documents, and can suggest new filenames. It runs on local models or a remote API, and Edit > Undo last run reverses the latest batch.

Choose AI File Sorter for a free, one-off cleanup when you are comfortable picking a local model. Weakness: audio and video are named from embedded metadata only, and the project does not document a watch folder.

## 7. Hazel, File Juggler, and DropIt: rules without AI

Hazel 6 ($42, Mac), File Juggler ($50 plus tax, Windows, version 3.1.9) and DropIt (free, Windows, last release February 2018) watch folders and act on conditions: name patterns, extensions, dates or matched text. Softpedia's editors rate Hazel 5.0/5 and File Juggler 4.5/5.

Weakness: none of them understands what a photo, scan or video shows, so every case needs its own rule. Zush covers the simple cases too: a prompt such as “Group receipts by year, then by month” or “PDFs to Documents, images to Pictures” sorts by date or type without a rule per case. Keep a rule engine for scripts, commands or archive extraction. See the [Hazel alternative](/hazel-alternative) page and [Hazel for Windows alternatives](/blog/hazel-for-windows-alternatives).

## Test any sorter on 20 files first

1. Copy 20 mixed files into a test folder: invoices, receipts, photos, a screenshot, a scan, a video.
2. Write one rule you actually need, for example "Group invoices by company and receipts by month."
3. Check the plan: are folder names consistent, and did every file land where you expected?
4. Apply, restart the app, then try undo.
5. Only then point the sorter at Downloads or a real archive.

## FAQ

### What is the best AI file sorter?

Zush is our pick for most people on Mac and Windows. You describe the folders in plain language or use Auto, and Zush reads each file, renames it and moves it into the right folder after you review the batch. It handles PDFs, scans, photos, video and audio, and works with local AI. Sortio is the closest alternative if you need Linux. We make Zush, so test both on copies.

### Can AI sort files into folders automatically?

Yes. Zush, Sortio Pro, RenameClick's Auto Flow, NameQuick and Sparkle can all move new files into folders without you starting each batch. In Zush, save your Folder rules in a Template and assign it to Monitor: every new file in that folder is renamed and sorted as it arrives. Test the Template on a manual batch first so you can check the folders it picks.

### Is there a free AI file sorter?

AI File Sorter is free and open source. Zush includes 50 free AI renames with no account, card or API key, enough for a real test of folder sorting. Sortio has a one-time AI trial plus free rule-based sorting, NameQuick gives 30 free renames, and RenameClick includes 100 free renames or moves a month after its local model download.

### Which AI file sorter gives the most for the money?

It depends on what is included. RenameClick's $36 plus VAT lifetime covers its local model or your own key, and Sortio Pro is $99 a year with an AI allowance. Zush's $48 one-time license includes the managed Zush Cloud AI as well as BYOK, Ollama and LM Studio, unlimited renames, future updates and up to 3 devices across Mac and Windows.

### Which AI file sorter works offline?

Zush sorts offline with LM Studio or Ollama on Mac and Windows: both file analysis and folder planning run on your computer. RenameClick bundles a local model (16 GB of RAM recommended), Sortio supports Ollama and LM Studio on every plan, including Free, and AI File Sorter runs on local models. In cloud mode, Zush sends file content to Zush Cloud AI or your own provider for analysis.

### What is the difference between an AI file sorter and a rules-based organizer?

A rules-based organizer such as Hazel or DropIt moves files that match conditions you write, like a filename pattern or extension. An AI file sorter such as Zush reads what the file contains and follows an instruction like "group invoices by company," so it can sort files whose names give no clue. Zush also follows a plain date or file-type instruction, so a rule engine is only needed for actions such as scripts or archive extraction.
