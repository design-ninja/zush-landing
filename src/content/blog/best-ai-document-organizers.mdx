## Two ways to organize documents with AI

The choice between these two groups matters more than any feature list:

1. **Folder-based tools** rename and move files where they already live. Your documents stay ordinary files in Finder, File Explorer, iCloud, OneDrive or Dropbox. Zush, NameQuick, Wisfile and File Juggler work this way.
2. **Archive tools** import documents into their own library or server, then add tags, full-text search and metadata. Paperless-ngx and DEVONthink work this way.

If you share folders with an accountant, a team or another app, choose a folder-based tool. If you want one place to search everything and are happy to work inside that app, choose an archive.

## 1. Zush: best AI document organizer for folders you already use

[Zush](/ai-file-organizer) runs on Mac and Windows. Drop in a folder of PDFs, scans, receipts, contracts, Word, Excel, Pages or EPUB files and it reads each one, including image-only scans with no text layer. It proposes a name built from the fields you search by: date, vendor, amount, client, document type, invoice number. Nothing changes until you click Rename, and you can double-click any suggestion to edit it.

![Zush for Mac showing each proposed filename for a mixed folder before the batch is applied](/images/showcase/macos/batch-rename-light.webp)

Names follow a Template, so every invoice looks the same instead of whatever the AI improvises. Bookkeeper Expenses is one of the ready-made Templates, and 145+ Naming Blocks let you build your own pattern, with sequence numbers if you need them.

To file documents too, write a sentence in **Folder rules**, such as "Group invoices by company and receipts by month," or leave it on Auto. You review names and folders in one preview. Rules can also use file details such as dates and page counts, and you can sort without renaming, on Mac and Windows.

![Zush Folder rules dialog with the instruction to group invoices by company and receipts by month, plus example rules](/images/showcase/macos/folder-rules-light.webp)

For incoming paperwork, assign the Template to **Monitor** on a scanner or Downloads folder. AI Rename also detects duplicate scans: keep them, mark them in the name, or move them to the Trash or Recycle Bin, and copies use no credits. Rename History persists after a restart, and Undo All restores a batch and flags conflicts.

It works right away: 50 free AI renames on Zush Cloud AI, with no account, card, API key or model download. Sensitive files can stay on your computer with Ollama or LM Studio. The $48 one-time license includes the managed cloud AI plus every local and BYOK mode, all future updates, up to 3 devices across Mac and Windows, and a 14-day refund.

Choose Zush if your documents should remain normal files that anyone can open from a shared folder. ## 2. Paperless-ngx: best free self-hosted archive

Paperless-ngx (v3.4.2) is an open-source document management system (GPL-3.0) that you run on your own server or NAS, usually with Docker. It runs OCR with Tesseract in more than 100 languages, keeps the original next to a PDF/A archive copy, and uses machine learning to suggest tags, correspondents and document types. It can also import documents from email.

Its AI features are optional and off by default: AI suggestions, chat with your documents, and similar-document search. They run locally through Ollama or through an OpenAI-compatible API. Paperless manages filenames and storage paths itself, with configurable formats.

Choose Paperless-ngx if you are comfortable self-hosting and want a searchable paper archive for a household or small office. Weakness: it needs a server and Docker setup, and documents live in its archive rather than your existing folder structure.

## 3. DEVONthink: best document database for Mac

DEVONthink 4.4.2 is a long-running Mac app for storing, searching and classifying documents. OCR and generative AI features (chat, summaries, tagging, smart-rule batch processing) are in the Pro and Server editions only. Supported models include ChatGPT, Claude and Mistral, plus local models through Ollama or LM Studio.

Standard costs $99, Pro $199 and Server $499, each with two Mac seats and one year of updates. The license never expires, and another year of updates costs $44, $89 or $134.

Choose DEVONthink if you want a research-grade document library on a Mac. Weakness: reading scans needs at least the $199 Pro edition, and your documents live inside its database, which is more than most people need to name and file paperwork.

## 4. NameQuick: document renaming with conditional rules

NameQuick focuses on recurring PDFs, scans, receipts, invoices, contracts, Word documents and spreadsheets on Mac and Windows. It renames by content with Presets, watches folders, and runs conditional Rules that move, tag or archive files after naming.

![NameQuick promo image showing scanned invoices, receipts, and contracts renamed with dates and vendor names](/images/blog/competitors/namequick.webp)

You get 30 free Managed renames with no email or card. Managed plans run from $12 per month for 500 renames to $99 per month for 10,000, and rename packs cost $19 for 500 or $39 for 1,500 (valid 12 months). The $69 Self-Managed license covers one computer and needs your own API key or a local model.

Choose NameQuick for a document workflow built on if/then Rules. Weakness: its one-time license does not include managed AI, and undo can fail if a file was moved afterwards. Full comparison: [Zush vs NameQuick](/blog/zush-vs-namequick).

## 5. Wisfile: free local renaming for PDFs and Word

Wisfile is free and runs locally on Mac and Windows. It renames PDFs and Word documents from their content, for example `Author-Year-Title` for research papers, and can group files into topic folders.

![Wisfile's own illustration of random PDF names turned into dated, descriptive document filenames](/images/blog/competitors/wisfile.webp)

Choose Wisfile for a one-off cleanup of a document archive on no budget. Weakness: according to its FAQ there is no folder monitoring, no scheduling and no built-in undo, so work on copies. Details: [Zush vs Wisfile](/blog/zush-vs-wisfile).

## 6. File Juggler: rules for predictable documents on Windows

File Juggler is not AI, but it routes documents that always look the same. Rules can match words inside PDF, Word and Excel files, reuse dates found in the text, then rename and move the file. Version 3.1.9 shipped October 30, 2026, and Softpedia's editors rate it 4.5/5.

It costs $50 one-time before tax, with a 30-day full trial. Weakness: it cannot read image-only scans without a separate OCR program. If your documents vary, see [File Juggler alternatives](/blog/file-juggler-alternatives).

## FAQ

### What is the best AI document organizer?

Zush, for documents that should stay in ordinary folders on Mac or Windows. It names and sorts PDFs, scans and Office files by content, with a preview before anything changes and persistent undo. Paperless-ngx is the best free choice if you want a self-hosted archive, and DEVONthink Pro suits Mac users who want a full document database. We make Zush, so test it on copies of your own paperwork.

### Is there a free AI document organizer?

Yes. Paperless-ngx is free and open source but needs a server. Wisfile is free for PDF and Word documents on Mac and Windows, without undo. Zush includes 50 free AI renames with no account, card or API key, and NameQuick includes 30 free Managed renames. DEVONthink and File Juggler offer trials rather than a free tier.

### Can an AI document organizer read scanned PDFs?

Yes, but not all of them. Zush reads image-only scans directly, with no separate OCR step. Paperless-ngx runs Tesseract OCR on import, and DEVONthink runs OCR only in its Pro and Server editions. NameQuick and Wisfile also handle scans. File Juggler needs a separate OCR program first, because its rules only match text that already exists in the file.

### Can I organize documents with AI without uploading them?

Yes. Zush with Ollama or LM Studio, Wisfile, Paperless-ngx with a local Ollama backend, DEVONthink Pro with a local model, and NameQuick Self-Managed with a local model all keep documents on your own hardware. In Zush Cloud AI or BYOK mode, document content is sent to the cloud model for analysis, so pick the local mode for confidential files.

### What does a one-time document organizer license include?

It varies a lot. DEVONthink's $99 to $499 licenses include one year of updates. NameQuick's $69 Self-Managed license covers one computer and needs your own AI. File Juggler's $50 buys rules without AI. Zush's $48 license includes the managed Zush Cloud AI, local and BYOK modes, unlimited renames, all future updates and up to 3 devices across Mac and Windows.
