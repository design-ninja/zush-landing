![Zush AI rename results on macOS showing content-aware filenames for mixed files](/images/showcase/macos/batch-rename-light.webp)

Zush runs on both Mac (macOS 15+) and Windows (10/11) and offers two PRO plans: $10/month or $48 one-time, both with unlimited PRO renames. Its strongest advantage is naming control: built-in Templates for common jobs, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, prompts, formatting, localization, Finder tags, Spotlight metadata, and per-folder Template assignments. Rename History keeps the original and new name, folder, and timestamp and can revert an applied batch. Version 3.0 also supports 100+ formats across images, RAW, design, documents, video, and audio.

Both products offer local-model paths, so local AI is useful for privacy but does not decide this comparison.

### Folder grouping versus conditional actions

Zush offers **AI folder sorting (beta)** using **Auto** or plain-language **Folder rules**, such as “Group invoices by company and receipts by month.” It proposes groups from content while a Template controls the filenames. NameQuick also has Quick Organize for an approved folder plan, plus conditional Rules to move files, adjust dates, and archive them, with Finder tags and colors on Mac. Content-based grouping is not exclusive to Zush; compare the filename controls and the conditional actions your workflow needs.

Choose a **Destination folder**, or keep the source folder as the root. Enable **Reuse existing folders** to prefer suitable folders already there; Zush can create a new one when nothing fits. In a manual batch, review both the suggested names and folders before applying. To rename in place, leave sorting off and keep the original destination.

Save the sorting settings in a **Template** and assign it to **Monitor** for new files directly inside the watched folder. This does not reorganize files already processed. Test the Template in a manual batch first. See the [folder sorting guide](/docs/folder-sorting) for the setup and monitoring scope.

NameQuick publishes Windows and Mac downloads with two purchase paths. Managed is $12/$29/$59/$99 per month for 500/2,000/5,000/10,000 suggestions, with no device limit. Self-Managed is $69 once for one computer, lifetime updates, and your own key or model. Its Windows FAQ confirms Managed, BYOK, Ollama, and LM Studio; MLX and Finder tags/comments are Mac-only. The Windows installer is x64, with ARM PCs using Windows 11 emulation.

NameQuick publicly lists OpenAI, OpenRouter, Claude, Gemini, Ollama, LM Studio, and MLX for its self-managed workflow. Its privacy statements, provider availability, and Windows feature support are vendor-published claims and should be checked against the current build before purchase.

Its published workflow includes Watch Folders, connected inboxes, Undo & Recovery, Rules, Quick Organize, and a local content-search index. These are substantive document-workflow features. Its 30+ format claim still does not establish support for each camera RAW or design format listed by Zush.

### Recent releases and meaningful limitations

The changelog lists **3.0.16 on September 28**: model and reasoning selections persist across switches/restarts, and custom providers save API keys correctly. It now lists **3.0.17 on September 30** for Mac and Windows, with onboarding/trial changes and reliability fixes. Trial wording differs between release notes and the current product page; the allowance and card requirements above follow the product page checked October 5. Confirm them in the actual signup screen.

Managed allowance is consumed when a suggestion is generated, even if you never apply it. Unused monthly renames expire; annual billing supplies the year's allowance upfront. The Self-Managed license covers one computer, and local photos/scans require a vision-capable model. These are more concrete constraints than treating Windows support as undocumented.

## Format support

### RAW photos: Zush yes, NameQuick unclear

Zush publishes a complete RAW format list (CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, DNG, ORF, RAF, RW2, PEF, SRW, SR2). NameQuick lists "30+ file formats" on their site but does not enumerate camera RAW formats specifically. If you shoot RAW, Zush is the safer pick.

### Office docs and OCR: both cover the common case

NameQuick advertises OCR for scanned docs and EXIF extraction for photos. Zush supports PDF, DOCX, XLSX, PPT, PPTX, TXT, MD, JSON, EML, CSV, DOC, plus iWork files such as Pages, Numbers, and Keynote. Both handle the everyday office formats.

### Audio / video / archives

NameQuick mentions audio, video, and archives as part of its 30+ formats. Zush 3.0 supports common video files, subtitle sidecars, design files, iWork documents, and audio files including MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC, OGG, WebM, and MPGA. NameQuick is still broader if ZIP/archive renaming is central to your workflow.

## FAQ

### Can Zush sort files into folders as well as rename them?

Yes. Its optional AI folder sorting is in beta on Mac and Windows. Choose Auto or write Folder rules, select the destination, and review both names and groups in a manual batch. A sorting-enabled Template can also process new files directly inside a monitored folder; it does not reorganize files already processed. Leave sorting off and keep the original destination for naming only.

### Does NameQuick run on Windows?

Yes. Its Windows FAQ documents Managed, BYOK, Ollama, and LM Studio. MLX, Finder tags, and Finder comments are Mac-only. The installer is x64 and Windows 11 ARM uses emulation. Zush also documents Mac and Windows desktop workflows; compare the platform-specific actions you actually need.

### Is BYOK free in both tools?

In Zush, BYOK is available on every plan — no separate BYOK purchase. In NameQuick, the public Self-Managed plan is $69 one-time, after which you bring your own API key or local model.

### Does Zush support all NameQuick AI providers?

No. Zush supports managed cloud, BYOK with Gemini, OpenRouter, OpenAI, and Claude, plus two local routes: LM Studio and Ollama. NameQuick publicly lists OpenAI, OpenRouter, Claude, Gemini, Ollama, LM Studio, and MLX. NameQuick remains broader only where MLX is a hard requirement.

### Does NameQuick support RAW photos?

NameQuick's public format list says "30+ formats" without enumerating RAW camera variants. Zush explicitly lists 11 RAW formats. Photographers who rely on RAW are safer with Zush.

### Which has better naming control?

Zush publishes a granular catalog of 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, formatting, localization, metadata, and per-folder Templates. NameQuick documents presets, prompts, conditional Rules, Quick Organize, and Mac Finder actions. Zush is the documented fit for composing exact fields; NameQuick has additional document-search and conditional-action workflows. Neither claim establishes measured naming accuracy.
