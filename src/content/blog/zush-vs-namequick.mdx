## Zush

![Zush AI rename results on macOS showing content-aware filenames for mixed files](/images/showcase/macos/batch-rename-light.webp)

Zush runs on Mac (macOS 15+) and Windows 10/11. The $48 one-time license includes managed Zush Cloud AI, so you don't need an API key or a local model to get unlimited renames. BYOK, Ollama, and LM Studio are in the same license, along with all future updates and up to 3 devices across Mac and Windows. Refunds are available for 14 days.

Its main strength is naming control: ready-made Templates (Movies & TV, Remove Duplicates and others), 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, sequence numbers with a custom start and leading zeros, formatting, localization, Finder tags, and Spotlight metadata. You see every suggested name before anything changes, can double-click a name to edit it, and nothing is renamed until you click Rename. Rename History keeps the original and new name, folder, and timestamp, and can revert a whole batch.

Recent releases added video naming from speech, embedded subtitles and metadata, including MKV files. Local speech recognition works with Ollama or LM Studio. [Duplicate detection](/remove-duplicate-files) now runs inside AI Rename: keep copies, mark them in the name, or move them to the Trash. Copies use no credits, and Undo restores them.

You can [export Templates](/docs/template-import-export) with Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, and folder rules, then import them on another computer. Choose local destinations, folder permissions, and AI connections again there; this transfers the workflow settings, not your files.

### Folder grouping versus conditional actions

Zush offers **AI folder sorting** using **Auto** or plain-language **Folder rules**, such as "Group invoices by company and receipts by month." It can also sort into nested folders without renaming, using custom rules on size, dates, camera, duration, or page count. NameQuick has Quick Organize for an approved folder plan, plus conditional Rules to move files, adjust dates, and archive them, with Finder tags and colors on Mac. Content-based grouping is not exclusive to Zush; compare the filename controls and the conditional actions your workflow needs.

Choose a **Destination folder**, or keep the source folder as the root. Enable **Reuse existing folders** to prefer suitable folders already there; Zush can create a new one when nothing fits. To rename in place, leave sorting off and keep the original destination.

Save the sorting settings in a **Template** and assign it to **Monitor** for new files in the watched folder. On Mac, monitoring also covers external drives and resumes after you reconnect them. Changing the Template does not reorganize files already processed. See the [folder sorting guide](/docs/folder-sorting) for the monitoring scope.

NameQuick's latest release is **3.0.57 (November 12, 2026)** for Mac and Windows, and the Mac app is now also on the Mac App Store. It has two ways to pay. Managed is a monthly plan with a rename allowance, or a one-time rename pack valid for 12 months. Self-Managed is $69 once for one computer, Mac or Windows, with lifetime updates and your own key or local model. NameQuick's own docs say AI costs are separate on that path.

Its AI menu has grown. Beyond OpenAI, OpenRouter, Claude, Gemini, Ollama, LM Studio, and MLX, it now lists Mistral and university gateways. MLX and Finder tags/comments are Mac-only.

**Choose NameQuick if** your work is mostly documents. Email invoice import, a local content-search index, a duplicate finder, Watch Folders, Rules, and Quick Organize are real strengths that Zush doesn't match one for one. The 30 free Managed renames are as easy to start as Zush's free tier.

### The limits to know before buying

Managed allowance is used when a suggestion is generated, before you apply it. NameQuick's documentation says rejected suggestions count, suggestions that can't be written to disk count, and renaming the same file again counts; failed processing and Preset tests do not. Unused monthly renames don't carry over. With your own key, the provider may still bill every call.

On Windows, the installer is x64 only (ARM PCs run it through emulation) and it is unsigned, so Microsoft SmartScreen shows a warning before it installs. Undo can fail if a file was moved after renaming.

## Format support

### RAW photos: Zush yes, NameQuick unclear

Zush publishes its RAW list (CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, DNG, ORF, RAF, RW2, PEF, SRW, SR2). NameQuick lists "30+ file formats" but does not name camera RAW formats. If you shoot RAW, test your own files in NameQuick before buying.

### Office docs and OCR: both cover the common case

NameQuick advertises OCR for scanned documents, EXIF extraction for photos, and importing invoices from email. Zush supports PDF, DOCX, XLSX, PPT, PPTX, TXT, MD, JSON, EML, CSV, DOC, EPUB, and iWork files such as Pages, Numbers, and Keynote.

### Audio, video and archives

Zush names videos from what is said in them, from embedded subtitles, and from metadata, and that includes MKV. It also covers audio (MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC, OGG, WebM, MPGA), subtitle sidecars, and design files. NameQuick mentions audio, video, and archives among its 30+ formats. If ZIP or archive renaming is central to your work, NameQuick covers it and Zush doesn't.

## AI provider menu

NameQuick lists OpenAI, OpenRouter, Claude, Gemini, Mistral, university gateways, Ollama, LM Studio, and MLX, plus its own Managed service. Zush offers managed Zush Cloud AI, BYOK with Gemini, OpenRouter, OpenAI, or Claude, and local LM Studio or Ollama.

- **OpenRouter**: both support it, so it doesn't separate them.
- **Managed cloud on a one-time license**: Zush includes it in the $48 license. NameQuick's managed AI is monthly or a 12-month pack; its one-time license is BYOK or local only.
- **Mistral, university gateways, MLX**: only NameQuick. MLX matters if Apple Silicon-optimized local inference is a hard requirement.

## FAQ

### When is Zush a good alternative to NameQuick?

Zush fits mixed-file Mac and Windows work: RAW photos, design files, video named from speech or subtitles, iWork documents, and PDFs in the same folder. Its $48 one-time license includes managed cloud AI and covers up to 3 devices, and Rename History lets you undo whole batches. NameQuick is the better fit when email invoice import, content search, or MLX matter more than format coverage.

### Can I try NameQuick without a credit card?

Yes. NameQuick Managed starts with 30 free renames and asks for neither an email nor a card. The longer 14-day Managed trial, with 70 renames, does require a card. The Self-Managed trial uses your own key or local model and asks for no card. Zush gives 50 free AI renames on its managed cloud, with no account, card, or API key.

### Which is better value: Zush $48 or NameQuick $69?

It depends on what you need the license to include. Zush's $48 one-time license includes managed Zush Cloud AI, BYOK, and local models, unlimited renames, all future updates, and up to 3 Mac or Windows devices. NameQuick's $69 Self-Managed license covers one computer with lifetime updates, but you supply the AI through your own key or a local model, and those costs are separate.

### Where are my files processed in each app?

In Zush, Zush Cloud AI sends file content to the cloud for naming, BYOK sends it to the provider you choose, and Ollama or LM Studio keep it on your computer. NameQuick works the same way in principle: Managed uses its cloud, Self-Managed uses your key or a local model such as Ollama, LM Studio, or MLX. For fully local work, use a local model in either app.

### Does NameQuick run on Windows?

Yes. NameQuick ships a Windows build with Managed, BYOK, Ollama, and LM Studio. MLX and Finder tags or comments are Mac-only. The Windows installer is x64, so ARM PCs run it through emulation, and it is unsigned, which means SmartScreen warns you before installing. Zush supports Windows 10 and 11 and Macs with Apple Silicon or Intel.

### Does Zush support all NameQuick AI providers?

No. Zush supports its managed cloud, BYOK with Gemini, OpenRouter, OpenAI, and Claude, plus LM Studio and Ollama for local work. NameQuick also lists Mistral, university gateways, and MLX on Mac. If one of those is a hard requirement, NameQuick is the better match. If you just want AI that works without setup, both have a managed option.

### Does NameQuick support RAW photos?

NameQuick's public format list says "30+ formats" without naming camera RAW variants. Zush lists 11 RAW formats, including CR3, NEF, ARW, DNG, and RAF. If RAW files are central to your work, test a few of your own in NameQuick's free renames before paying, or start with Zush, where RAW support is documented.

## Verdict

Both tools have BYOK, local-model options, folder monitoring, undo, and content-aware naming. The decision comes down to what you're paying for and what you rename:

- For a one-time license that includes a managed cloud, covers 3 devices, and handles RAW, design, and video files with persistent batch undo: <BlogHighlight>Zush at $48 one-time or $10/month</BlogHighlight>.
- For document-heavy work with email invoice import, content search, MLX, or Mistral: NameQuick.

Start with Zush's 50 free renames on a test folder before paying for either.
