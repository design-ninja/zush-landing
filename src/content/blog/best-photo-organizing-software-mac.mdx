## How we compared Mac photo organizers

We compared each app against six jobs that matter after the first import:

1. **Library model:** managed library, catalog, ordinary Finder folders, or a mix.
2. **Retrieval:** albums, people, dates, places, metadata, natural-language search, or filenames.
3. **RAW workflow:** whether the app is built for large camera imports and professional selection.
4. **Device access:** Mac only, Apple devices, cloud sync, or private multi-device sync.
5. **File control:** whether original folders stay visible and portable outside the app.
6. **Cost and exit risk:** subscription, one-time license, optional storage, and what still works if you stop paying.

The comparison is based on published capabilities. Search quality depends on your library, language, file formats and existing metadata, so test the finalists on a copied folder before moving an archive.

## 1. Apple Photos: best free choice for personal libraries

**Best for:** iPhone and Mac users who want a free library with iCloud sync and almost no setup.

[Apple Photos](https://support.apple.com/guide/photos/welcome/mac) groups images by time, people, pets, places, trips, albums and recognized subjects. In macOS 26 the **Duplicates** collection sits under **Library** in the sidebar. It finds duplicate photos and videos and merges them, keeping one copy with the combined metadata.

Photos stores imported images inside a Photos library by default. That saves you from daily file handling, but the library should be used through Photos, not edited by hand in Finder. Apple supports extra libraries and referenced files, although only the System Photo Library takes part fully in iCloud Photos.

**Watch out for:** iCloud Photos is sync, not a second backup. A deletion syncs to every device. Keep a separate backup of the library or of exported originals. Referenced files need their own backup too, and moving them in Finder can break the link in Photos.

<span id="3-adobe-lightroom-best-for-editing-and-cloud-access"></span>

## 2. Adobe Lightroom: best for editing and cloud access

**Best for:** photographers who want editing, search and a synced library in one subscription.

Adobe Lightroom combines editing with albums, stacks, ratings, flags, keywords, metadata, People view, and search by content or plain-language description. The Lightroom plan costs $11.99 per month on an annual commitment with 1TB of cloud storage. The Photography 1TB plan costs $19.99 per month.

Lightroom fits when one app must ingest, edit, organize, sync and deliver images. A wedding photographer can rate a shoot, filter by camera or lens, collect selects in an album, edit the keepers, and open the same library on another device.

Lightroom and Lightroom Classic are different library models. Lightroom keeps a synced cloud library. Lightroom Classic is a desktop catalog with more control over folders on disk. Decide which one you want before importing a large archive.

**Watch out for:** there is no one-time purchase, and Adobe's People view documentation says face analysis for synced Lightroom libraries runs in the cloud. Privacy-sensitive archives may fit a local catalog better.

<span id="4-mylio-photos-best-for-private-multi-device-libraries"></span>

## 3. Mylio Photos: best for private multi-device libraries

**Best for:** large family archives spread over Macs, PCs, phones and external drives, without a cloud upload.

Mylio Photos builds one library from photos and videos stored across computers, phones, tablets and attached drives. It can keep your existing folder structure, connect devices peer to peer, and keep small previews on phones while the originals stay on storage you control.

Organizing tools include folders, albums, face tagging, keywords, metadata, a map, and the LifeCalendar view. Mylio also finds duplicates and similar photos. Its own materials say face recognition and other analysis run on your devices.

The base app is free to start. Paid plans add wider sync, protection and backup. Mylio announced its Photos+ plan at $119.99 per year in 2024. Its current plans page shows Personal and Business offers without prices, so check the price at checkout.

**Watch out for:** multi-device sync still needs a storage plan. Small previews are not your originals, and a synced copy is not an offline backup.

<span id="5-capture-one-pro-best-for-raw-editing-and-tethered-shoots"></span>

## 4. Capture One Pro: best for RAW editing and tethered shoots

**Best for:** professional RAW work, tethered studio shoots, and project-by-project organization.

Capture One Pro is mainly a RAW editor and tethering tool, but its organizing is solid. You choose between long-lived Catalogs and project-based Sessions, then use albums, Smart Albums, filters, ratings, color tags, keywords and culling tools.

Sessions keep each job portable and self-contained. Catalogs are better for searching a whole archive. That split suits commercial, studio, fashion, product and event photographers.

Capture One sells a subscription with monthly or annual billing and a perpetual Pro license. Its pricing page does not show amounts until checkout. Capture One describes the perpetual license as best for photographers who don't depend on the newest updates or hardware support.

**Watch out for:** it is more than a family library needs. Its strengths are editing and capture, not automatic recognition of decades of snapshots.

<span id="6-excire-foto-2027-best-for-local-ai-catalog-search"></span>

## 5. Excire Foto 2027: best for local AI catalog search

**Best for:** private local archives where you want AI search and a one-time price.

Excire Foto 2027 is a standalone photo and video manager built around AI search. It finds content, faces, scenes, visible text, moods, activities and similar images without manual tags on every file. It also has duplicate search, automatic keywords, map and timeline views, and tools for rating or culling a shoot.

Excire says its analysis runs locally and photos are not sent to third parties. Excire Foto 2027 costs $249 as a one-time purchase, with a 14-day trial.

**Watch out for:** it adds another catalog and preview database that needs disk space and its own backup. It finds files but does not change their names. Check the current macOS and processor requirements before buying.

## 6. Tonfotos: best for a family archive on any desktop

**Best for:** families who want face tagging and a family tree, with a free tier and a lifetime license.

[Tonfotos](https://tonfotos.com/) arranges photos by year and date, finds duplicates, recognizes faces, and shows geotagged photos on a map. You can link the people you tag into a family tree. It treats your computer, drives and NAS as one archive, and albums group photos without moving them. It runs on macOS, Windows and Linux.

The Free version has the paid features but limits how many people you can tag, and it collects anonymous usage statistics. Personal costs $59 for one device and Family $159 for up to 5 devices. Both are lifetime licenses.

**Watch out for:** Tonfotos's homepage does not say whether face recognition runs on your computer or in the cloud. Check its privacy policy if that matters for your archive.

## 7. Zush: best for naming and sorting photo files outside Photos

**Best for:** photos in ordinary Finder folders, client folders, exports or external drives that need descriptive names and a sensible folder layout.

Zush is not a photo library. It proposes descriptive filenames and optional content-based folders, and nothing changes until you click Rename. It does not offer albums, face browsing, editing, culling or cloud photo storage. If you need those, pick one of the apps above and use Zush alongside it.

### Sort photo files into folders with AI

[Zush](/ai-file-sorter) can rename supported files and group them into real folders by content in the same batch. Leave **Folder rules** empty for Auto grouping, or describe the groups in plain language. For photos, try:

> Group images by subject, such as People, Food, and Landscapes.

1. Add a small copied batch in **AI Rename** and select a Template.
2. Turn on **+ Sort** and choose the **Destination folder**, the root for the sorted subfolders. Without a separate destination, the source folder stays the root.
3. Enter the grouping instruction in **Folder rules**. **Naming Blocks** set the filename format separately.
4. Turn on **Reuse existing folders** if suitable groups already exist in the destination. Zush creates a new folder when none fits.
5. Review the proposed names and folders, fix anything wrong, then apply.

![Zush for Mac previewing content-based folder groups and proposed filenames before Rename & sort](/images/showcase/macos/folder-sorting-light.webp)

Folder rules can also use nested folders, such as year and then month, and custom rules based on size, dates, camera or duration. You can also sort already renamed files without renaming them again or spending another rename credit. Save the setup in a Template and assign it to **Monitor** to handle new files arriving in a watched folder, including on an external drive; monitoring resumes when the drive reconnects. Monitoring does not reorganize files it already processed.

Turn on **Detect duplicate files** in the Template to catch exact copies in the batch: keep them, mark them in the name, or move them to the Trash. Copies use no credits, and Undo restores them. This matches identical files only, not similar shots, so it does not replace the Duplicates collection in Photos. See [removing duplicate files](/remove-duplicate-files).

Use exported images or a copied Finder folder. Do not reorganize a Photos library package, and for originals tracked by a catalog, follow its rename or relink workflow. The [step-by-step Mac sorting guide](/blog/sort-files-into-folders-with-ai-mac) and the [photo renaming guide](/blog/how-to-rename-images-with-ai-on-macos) cover both steps in detail.

## Photo Mechanic Plus: no longer sold

Photo Mechanic Plus added a catalog to Photo Mechanic's ingest and selection workflow, spanning many folders, drives and offline media, with search by IPTC metadata, filters and saved searches.

Camera Bits stopped selling Photo Mechanic Plus to new buyers in February 2026 after catalog stability problems. Existing users should talk to Camera Bits support about migration. ## Which Mac photo organizer should you choose?

- **Apple Photos** if your library comes mostly from an iPhone and you want albums, people, places and shared libraries with minimal setup.
- **Lightroom** if editing and cloud delivery belong in the same subscription.
- **Mylio Photos** if one private library must span devices and drives without depending on one cloud.
- **Capture One Pro** if tethering, RAW editing, color work and culling drive the workflow.
- **Excire Foto** if you want local AI search and a one-time price.
- **Tonfotos** if you want a free family archive with a family tree, or need Linux.
- **Zush** if photos in Finder folders need descriptive names and content-based folders, with a preview before anything changes.

Many photographers end up with a pair: a library for search, culling, editing or sync, and descriptive filenames that still make sense in Finder, on external drives and in client handoffs. If you combine them, rename before importing where possible.

## A safe test before moving your whole library

Copy 50 representative images into a test folder: recent iPhone HEIC files, older JPEGs, a few RAW formats, edited exports, screenshots, Live Photos if relevant, and some files with weak or missing dates.

Then check:

1. Can the app find a photo by person, place, date, subject, rating and filename?
2. Does it keep RAW files, sidecars, edits, Live Photos and metadata intact?
3. Can you export originals without losing the folder or filename context you need?
4. What happens when a source drive is disconnected or a file moves?
5. Is there a documented backup and restore path for the originals and the catalog?
6. Will the price still make sense as the library and device count grow?

Keep the old library unchanged until the new one has survived an import, search, edit, export, backup and restore test.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best photo organizing software for Mac?

Apple Photos is the best starting point for most personal Mac libraries because it is free, built in, and syncs with iPhone. Mylio suits a private library across devices and drives. Lightroom and Capture One suit editing-led work. Excire Foto is best for local AI search with a one-time price, and Tonfotos for a family archive. Zush names and sorts photo files in ordinary Finder folders without creating a new catalog.

### What is the best free photo organizer for Mac?

Apple Photos is included with macOS and covers albums, people, places and duplicates. Tonfotos has a free version with its full feature set, limited only in how many people you can tag. Mylio is free to start for a private library across devices. Zush includes 50 free renames for testing descriptive filenames on a copied folder, but it is a renamer and sorter, not a free library manager.

### What is the best photo organizer without a subscription?

Apple Photos costs nothing beyond optional iCloud storage. Excire Foto 2027 is $249 one-time for local AI search. Tonfotos Personal is $59 for a lifetime license. Capture One sells a perpetual Pro license alongside its subscription. Zush is $48 one-time, and that license includes its managed Zush Cloud AI, all future updates, and up to 3 Mac or Windows devices. Lightroom has no one-time option.

### Which Mac photo organizers keep my photos on my own computer?

Apple Photos analyzes faces and content on the device, though iCloud Photos uploads the library if you turn it on. Mylio and Excire say their analysis runs on your devices. Lightroom's face analysis for synced libraries runs in Adobe's cloud. Tonfotos does not say where face recognition runs. Zush sends photos to Zush Cloud AI by default, or keeps analysis local when you pick Ollama or LM Studio.

### Is Apple Photos good for professional photographers?

Apple Photos can handle selects, albums and delivery for lighter professional work. Lightroom and Capture One go much further with RAW editing, culling and tethering, and standard Photo Mechanic handles fast ingest and IPTC metadata. Zush is useful next to any of them when descriptive filenames need to survive exports, client handoffs, external drives and a later change of catalog.

### Can AI organize photos stored on an external drive?

Yes. Mylio, Capture One, Excire and Tonfotos can catalog images on external drives, with different tradeoffs for sync, editing and AI search. Zush can rename and sort supported photos on a connected external drive, and its Monitor resumes when the drive reconnects. Keep an independent backup, and learn a catalog's relink process before renaming files that catalog already tracks.

### Should I organize photos by folders or use a catalog?

Use folders when portability, direct Finder access and simple backup matter most. Use a catalog when you need collections, ratings, face or content search, culling and editing without duplicating files. A durable setup combines both: a clear Year/Project folder structure under the catalog, plus descriptive filenames that still make sense if the catalog is ever unavailable or replaced.

## Verdict

For a personal Mac and iPhone library, start with Apple Photos and a separate backup. Choose Mylio when the archive must stay private across devices and drives, Lightroom or Capture One when editing defines the workflow, Excire for local AI search, and Tonfotos for a family archive with a lifetime license. Existing Photo Mechanic Plus users need a migration plan.

Choose Zush when photos in Finder, client folders, external drives or exports need descriptive filenames, content-based folders, or both. It works alongside a catalog and does not replace one.
