## What each tool actually is

### Zush: a desktop renamer with private modes

[Zush](/) installs on the Mac or PC where your files already live. Ready-made Templates, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, prompts, formatting, localization, tags and metadata define the convention; the AI fills content-aware fields instead of inventing the whole name. The same Template works in a reviewed batch or [folder monitoring](/docs/folder-monitoring). Rename History keeps original and new names, folder and timestamp and can restore an applied batch.

Four analysis modes set the privacy posture: Zush Cloud AI, BYOK through your own API account, LM Studio, and Ollama. The two local routes keep supported analysis on the machine.

### Renamed.to: a cloud service inside your cloud storage

[Renamed.to](https://www.renamed.to/) connects to Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive and renames documents where they live in cloud storage — files stay in your storage rather than moving to Renamed's servers, and confidence scoring routes uncertain names to review. Access is browser-first, with a REST API, CLI, watcher daemon, and Zapier/Make/n8n hooks for pipeline builders. Billing is per document: credit packs valid for 12 months, no subscription.

Its scope is narrow: document files in cloud storage. The main drawback is that photos, design files, audio, and video sit outside that scope, every processed document consumes credits, and the checked pages do not document a Zush-style post-apply Rename History.

## Pricing: unmetered vs per-file arithmetic

Renamed.to charges $0.0049–$0.009 per document. The important point is not that the unit price is low; it is that every run and re-run consumes credits. A bookkeeping practice processing 2,000 documents a month keeps paying for volume and pays again when a convention changes.

Zush's paid PRO is unmetered: $10/month while you need it, or <BlogHighlight>$48 once</BlogHighlight>. Re-running a folder because you changed the convention costs nothing, which changes how freely you iterate on Templates. Competitor sticker price is not treated as an advantage because it buys a narrower, metered workflow.

Both offer 50 free units to evaluate with.

## Formats: documents vs everything on disk

Renamed.to is document-scoped: PDFs, Office files, scanned images. If your renaming problem is invoices and contracts in a shared drive, that scope fits.

Zush covers the whole folder: the same invoice PDFs and scans, plus RAW and HEIC photos, screenshots, Sketch/Figma/PSD design files, iWork documents, audio (with transcription-based naming), and video. Mixed real-world folders — the Downloads directory, a client-project dump — stay one workflow instead of splitting into "documents here, everything else somewhere".

## Privacy: the structural difference

Renamed.to's model is cloud processing by design — your documents are analyzed by its service, inside your cloud storage, under its terms. For many teams that's acceptable; for regulated documents it's a real evaluation point.

Zush can run supported analysis locally through LM Studio or Ollama on Mac or Windows. The dedicated [medical records workflow](/for-medical) can use a local mode for scanner and fax folders; BYOK is the alternative when an organization routes analysis through its own provider account and key.

## Pick by use case

- **Bookkeeping practice living in Google Drive, documents only, API integration mandatory** → Renamed.to may fit that narrow requirement, but it remains cloud-only, document-scoped, and metered per file.
- **Invoices, receipts, and scans on a desktop, plus everything else in the folder** → [AI invoice renaming workflow](/rename-invoices-with-ai): one app, unmetered, undo, and the scanner workflow built in.
- **Regulated or confidential documents** → Zush Offline AI; cloud-only processing is the disqualifier on the other side.
- **API-first pipeline automation in cloud storage** → consider Renamed.to only when REST/CLI integration is mandatory and its cloud-only processing, document scope, and missing post-apply history are acceptable.
- **Photos, design files, audio, video in the mix** → Zush; they're outside Renamed.to's document scope.

## FAQ

### Is Zush cheaper than Renamed.to?

At sustained volume, yes: Zush PRO is $48 one-time unmetered, while Renamed.to bills every document (about $5–9 per thousand). At very low occasional volume Renamed.to's pay-as-you-go can cost less than a subscription — though Zush's 50 free renames cover light use too.

### Does Renamed.to have a desktop app?

No — it's web-first with cloud-storage integrations, an API, a CLI, and a watcher daemon. Zush is a native Mac and Windows desktop app.

### Can Renamed.to rename files offline or locally?

No, processing is cloud-based. Zush offers LM Studio and Ollama so supported analysis can stay on the machine.

### Which is better for scanned documents?

Both read scans. The difference is where: Zush processes them on your machine with AI vision (and can watch the scanner folder), Renamed.to processes them in cloud storage. For sensitive scans, local processing is the safer default — see the [scanned documents guide](/blog/rename-scanned-documents-automatically).

### Do they rename photos, audio, or video?

Zush does — RAW/HEIC photos, audio with transcription, video, design files. Renamed.to scopes itself to document formats (PDF, DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, scanned images).

## Verdict

Zush is the default in this comparison because it gives the user more control and a safer complete workflow: ready-made Templates, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, formatting, localization, metadata, preview, and Rename History with batch revert across 100+ formats. Renamed.to is relevant only when cloud-drive API integration is mandatory; its disadvantages are cloud-only processing, document-only scope, per-file metering, and no equivalent documented post-apply history.
