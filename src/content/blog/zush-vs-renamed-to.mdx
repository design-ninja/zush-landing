## What each tool actually is

### Zush: a desktop renamer with private modes

[Zush](/) installs on the Mac or PC where your files already live. It reads each file — text PDFs directly, [scans via AI vision](/rename-scanned-documents), photos, design files, audio, video — and applies your naming convention through [Templates and Naming Blocks](/docs/naming-blocks), with batch preview and undo. [Folder monitoring](/docs/folder-monitoring) keeps Downloads or a scanner folder continuously named.

Three analysis modes set the privacy posture: managed cloud AI (default), BYOK through your own API account, and Offline AI with local Ollama models — the last one keeps supported files entirely on the machine.

### Renamed.to: a cloud service inside your cloud storage

[Renamed.to](https://www.renamed.to/) connects to Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive and renames documents where they live in cloud storage — files stay in your storage rather than moving to Renamed's servers, and confidence scoring routes uncertain names to review. Access is browser-first, with a REST API, CLI, watcher daemon, and Zapier/Make/n8n hooks for pipeline builders. Billing is per document: credit packs valid for 12 months, no subscription.

It is genuinely good at what it scopes itself to: document files, in the cloud, at volume, for teams that live in shared drives.

## Pricing: unmetered vs per-file arithmetic

Renamed.to's credits are cheap per unit ($0.0049–$0.009 per document) — the question is whether your volume is bounded. A bookkeeping practice running 2,000 documents a month spends roughly $10–18/month at pack rates, forever, and every re-run of a batch (new convention, fixed template) bills again.

Zush's paid PRO is unmetered: $10/month while you need it, or <BlogHighlight>$48 once</BlogHighlight>. Re-running a folder because you changed the convention costs nothing, which changes how freely you iterate on Templates. For high-volume document work the one-time license crosses over within the first months; for occasional small batches, Renamed.to's pay-as-you-go can be the cheaper entry.

Both offer 50 free units to evaluate with.

## Formats: documents vs everything on disk

Renamed.to is document-scoped: PDFs, Office files, scanned images. If your renaming problem is invoices and contracts in a shared drive, that scope fits.

Zush covers the whole folder: the same invoice PDFs and scans, plus RAW and HEIC photos, screenshots, Sketch/Figma/PSD design files, iWork documents, audio (with transcription-based naming), and video. Mixed real-world folders — the Downloads directory, a client-project dump — stay one workflow instead of splitting into "documents here, everything else somewhere".

## Privacy: the structural difference

Renamed.to's model is cloud processing by design — your documents are analyzed by its service, inside your cloud storage, under its terms. For many teams that's acceptable; for regulated documents it's a real evaluation point.

Zush can run the entire pipeline locally: Offline AI mode analyzes supported files with local models on Mac or Windows, nothing transmitted. The dedicated [medical records workflow](/for-medical) uses that local mode for scanner and fax folders; BYOK is the alternative when an organization routes analysis through its own provider account and key.

## Pick by use case

- **Bookkeeping practice living in Google Drive, documents only, moderate volume** → Renamed.to's integrations fit naturally; compare credit spend against Zush's flat $48 at your volume.
- **Invoices, receipts, and scans on a desktop, plus everything else in the folder** → [Zush](/rename-invoices-with-ai): one app, unmetered, undo, and the scanner workflow built in.
- **Regulated or confidential documents** → Zush Offline AI; cloud-only processing is the disqualifier on the other side.
- **API-first pipeline automation in cloud storage** → Renamed.to's REST/CLI/watcher tooling is its strongest card.
- **Photos, design files, audio, video in the mix** → Zush; they're outside Renamed.to's document scope.

## FAQ

### Is Zush cheaper than Renamed.to?

At sustained volume, yes: Zush PRO is $48 one-time unmetered, while Renamed.to bills every document (about $5–9 per thousand). At very low occasional volume Renamed.to's pay-as-you-go can cost less than a subscription — though Zush's 50 free renames cover light use too.

### Does Renamed.to have a desktop app?

No — it's web-first with cloud-storage integrations, an API, a CLI, and a watcher daemon. Zush is a native Mac and Windows desktop app.

### Can Renamed.to rename files offline or locally?

No, processing is cloud-based. Zush's Offline AI mode runs local Ollama models so supported files never leave the machine.

### Which is better for scanned documents?

Both read scans. The difference is where: Zush processes them on your machine with AI vision (and can watch the scanner folder), Renamed.to processes them in cloud storage. For sensitive scans, local processing is the safer default — see the [scanned documents guide](/blog/rename-scanned-documents-automatically).

### Do they rename photos, audio, or video?

Zush does — RAW/HEIC photos, audio with transcription, video, design files. Renamed.to scopes itself to document formats (PDF, DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, scanned images).

## Verdict

Renamed.to is the strongest cloud-native, pay-per-document option in the field — right for teams whose documents live in shared drives and whose volume is occasional or API-driven. Zush is the desktop generalist: one unmetered license, every format on disk, scanner-to-archive automation, and the only fully local mode of the two. If your files are on a Mac or PC and privacy or volume matters, Zush is the default; if your world is a shared Google Drive, evaluate Renamed.to against the flat $48 and see which arithmetic wins.
