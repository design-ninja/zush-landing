## What each tool actually is

### Zush: a desktop renamer with private modes

[Zush](/) installs on the Mac or PC where your files already live, and the first 50 renames run on managed Zush Cloud AI with no account or setup. Ready-made Templates, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, prompts, formatting, localization, tags, and metadata define the convention; the AI fills content-aware fields instead of inventing the whole name. You preview every name, can double-click one to edit it, and nothing changes until you click Rename. The same Template works in a reviewed batch or in [folder monitoring](/docs/folder-monitoring). Rename History keeps original and new names, folder, and timestamp, and can restore a whole batch.

Four analysis modes set the privacy posture: Zush Cloud AI, BYOK through your own API account, LM Studio, and Ollama. The two local routes keep analysis on the machine. The $48 one-time license covers all four, all future updates, and up to 3 devices across Mac and Windows.

You can [export Templates](/docs/template-import-export) with Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, and folder rules, then import them on another computer.

### Sorting files on your computer

Zush includes **AI folder sorting** for desktop batches. Use **Auto** or write **Folder rules** such as "Group invoices by company and contracts by client." Folder rules control grouping separately from filename Templates. It can also sort into nested folders without renaming, using custom rules on size, dates, camera, duration, or page count. This works with files the desktop app can reach, including synced folders; it does not turn Zush into a cloud-storage API service like Renamed.to.

Save the sorting settings in a **Template** and assign it to **Monitor** for new files in the watched folder. On Mac, monitoring also covers external drives and resumes after you reconnect them. Changing the Monitor Template does not reorganize files already processed. See the [folder sorting guide](/docs/folder-sorting) for the setup.

### Renamed.to: a cloud service inside your cloud storage

Renamed.to connects to Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive and renames documents where they are stored. Files stay in your storage, and confidence scoring sends uncertain names to review. Access is browser-first, with a REST API (webhooks included), a CLI, a file watcher daemon for desktop and Linux, and n8n and Make listed as automation platforms. Zapier is listed on the homepage as an app connection but marked "Soon" on the pricing page, so check its status before relying on it. Billing is per file: credit packs valid for 12 months, no subscription.

**Choose Renamed.to if** your documents live in cloud storage and you need an API or CLI in a pipeline. That is a real strength Zush doesn't offer. The trade-off: photos, design files, audio, and video sit outside its scope, every processed document uses a credit, and its pages don't document a post-apply rename history.

## Pricing: unmetered vs per-file

Renamed.to works out to $0.0049 to $0.009 per document. The unit price is low, but every run and re-run uses credits, and the packs expire after 12 months. A bookkeeping practice processing 2,000 documents a month keeps paying for volume and pays again when a convention changes.

Zush's paid PRO is unmetered: $10/month while you need it, or <BlogHighlight>$48 once</BlogHighlight> with managed cloud AI included. Re-running a folder adds no Zush per-file charge. If you choose BYOK, your provider can still charge for the requests. See the [pricing page](/pricing) or [what AI file renaming costs](/blog/ai-file-renaming-cost) at different volumes.

Both offer 50 free units to evaluate with.

## Formats: documents vs everything on disk

Renamed.to is document-scoped: PDFs, Office files, scanned images. If your renaming problem is invoices and contracts in a shared drive, that scope fits.

Zush covers the whole folder: the same invoice PDFs and scans, plus RAW and HEIC photos, screenshots, Sketch/Figma/PSD design files, iWork and EPUB documents, audio, and video named from speech, embedded subtitles, and metadata, including MKV. Duplicate copies are detected during the rename and use no credits. A Downloads folder or a client-project dump stays one workflow instead of splitting into "documents here, everything else somewhere."

## Privacy: the structural difference

Renamed.to processes documents in the cloud by design, inside your cloud storage and under its terms. For many teams that's acceptable; for regulated documents it's a real evaluation point.

Zush can run analysis locally through LM Studio or Ollama on Mac or Windows; see the [offline AI file renamer](/offline-ai-file-renamer) page. The [medical records workflow](/for-medical) can use a local mode for scanner and fax folders. BYOK is the alternative when an organization routes analysis through its own provider account.

## Pick by use case

- **Documents in Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive, with an API or CLI requirement** → Renamed.to fits, as long as cloud-only processing and per-file billing are acceptable.
- **Invoices, receipts, and scans on a desktop, plus everything else in the folder** → Zush with the [AI invoice renaming workflow](/rename-invoices-with-ai): one app, unmetered, with undo.
- **Regulated or confidential documents** → Zush with a local model; Renamed.to has no local option.
- **A Linux server in the pipeline** → Renamed.to's watcher daemon runs on Linux; Zush has no Linux build.
- **Photos, design files, audio, or video in the mix** → Zush; they're outside Renamed.to's document scope.

For other document-focused options, see the [best AI document organizers](/blog/best-ai-document-organizers) or [Zush vs NameQuick](/blog/zush-vs-namequick).

## FAQ

### Is Zush cheaper than Renamed.to?

At steady volume, usually. Zush PRO is $48 one-time with no meter and includes managed cloud AI on up to 3 devices. Renamed.to bills every document, about $5 to $9 per thousand, and its credit packs expire after 12 months. For a few hundred documents a year, Renamed.to's $9 pack can cost less, though Zush's 50 free renames also cover very light use.

### Does Renamed.to have a desktop app?

Not as a full app. Renamed.to is web-first, with cloud-storage integrations, a REST API, a CLI, and a file watcher daemon that runs on desktop and Linux machines. Zush is a native Mac and Windows app where you preview names, edit them, and undo batches. If you need renaming inside a server pipeline, Renamed.to's tools fit that better.

### Where are my files processed?

Renamed.to processes documents in the cloud, and the files stay in your Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive storage. Zush lets you pick per job: managed Zush Cloud AI, your own API key with a provider you choose, or a local model in Ollama or LM Studio that keeps analysis on your computer. Renamed.to has no local option.

### Which is better for scanned documents?

Both read scans. The difference is where. Zush processes them locally when Ollama or LM Studio is selected and can watch the scanner folder for new files. Renamed.to processes them in the cloud inside your storage. For sensitive scans, local processing is the safer default. See the [scanned documents guide](/blog/rename-scanned-documents-automatically) for a full setup.

### Do they rename photos, audio, or video?

Zush does: RAW and HEIC photos, audio, design files, and video named from speech, embedded subtitles, and metadata, including MKV. Renamed.to limits itself to document formats such as PDF, Office files, and scanned images. If your folders mix documents with media, Zush keeps them in one workflow instead of two.

### Can I undo renames?

In Zush, yes. Rename History persists and can revert a whole batch; Undo All lists any conflicts and offers to restore under a new name. Renamed.to sends low-confidence names to review before applying and says originals are preserved, but its pages don't document a post-apply history or batch undo. Test on copies either way.

## Verdict

Zush is the stronger pick for most people renaming files on their own computer: ready-made Templates, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, preview, persistent Rename History with batch undo, a local AI option, and 100+ formats, with managed cloud AI included in the $48 license. Renamed.to makes sense when documents live in cloud storage and an API, CLI, or Linux watcher is a hard requirement, and per-file, cloud-only processing is acceptable.
