![Zush AI rename results showing files with descriptive before and after names and green checkmarks](/images/showcase/macos/batch-rename-light.webp)

Zush is a desktop AI file renamer for mixed folders. Its strongest advantage is the naming system: built-in Templates for common workflows, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, prompts, field order, case, separators, date formats, language, Finder tags, Spotlight metadata, and per-folder Template assignments. Rename History stores original and new names, folder, and timestamp and can revert an applied batch. Its 100+ formats span images, RAW, design, documents, iWork, subtitles, video, and audio.

The important part in this comparison is that Zush does not force a file-type silo. A folder with `IMG_4821.HEIC`, `_DSC0019.NEF`, `checkout-flow.fig`, `voice-memo.m4a`, `client-brief.pages`, `recording.mov`, and `invoice.pdf` can stay in one workflow.

Pricing is simple: $10/month or $48 one-time for PRO. Both paid plans remove the rename limit across all four modes: Zush Cloud AI, BYOK, LM Studio, and Ollama. Templates, Naming Blocks, and custom prompts are included free.

## RenameClick

![RenameClick desktop app interface (image: rename.click)](/images/blog/competitors/renameclick.webp)

RenameClick is a local-first desktop app for macOS and Windows. Its core pitch is privacy: after the one-time local model download, its built-in Qwen3-VL provider can process files on the device without an API key or internet connection. It also supports LM Studio and Ollama for local server workflows, plus OpenAI, Google Gemini, and Alibaba Cloud Qwen when you explicitly connect cloud API keys. RenameClick notes that cloud providers are not available in the Mac App Store version; use the direct download if those providers matter.

RenameClick is not only a renamer. It is closer to a file organizer: it can rename files, categorize them, move them into folders, run Auto Flow watch rules, search by content with AI Search, and build names from EXIF/date placeholders. Its supported-files documentation now covers three categories: common images (JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, BMP, SVG, HEIC, HEIF, TIFF), a long document list (PDF, DOC/DOCX, TXT, MD, CSV, RTF, ODT, JSON, XML, YAML, HTML, logs, SQL, SRT/VTT, ICS/VCF), and audio (MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, AAC) with local speech transcription. Video files, other audio codecs, archives, and executables are explicitly listed as unsupported.

## Format support

### Zush is broader for real mixed folders

Zush supports RAW photo formats, design files, audio, videos, iWork files, Office files, subtitles, and data/text formats. That is the practical advantage if your folders are messy because they are real: screenshots beside phone photos, camera RAWs beside PDFs, Figma/Sketch/PSD files beside Keynote/Pages documents, and video files beside meeting notes. Audio is no longer part of that gap — RenameClick handles common audio formats now — but RAW, design files, iWork, and video still are.

### RenameClick covers common images, a wide document list, and now audio

RenameClick covers common image and document workflows well — JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, BMP, SVG, HEIC, HEIF, TIFF, plus PDF, DOC/DOCX, TXT, Markdown, CSV, RTF, ODT, JSON, XML, YAML, HTML, logs, SQL, subtitles, and calendar/contact files — and it has added audio (MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, AAC) with local speech transcription. It also has useful document processing details: text extraction for text PDFs and Office documents, vision fallback for scanned PDFs, and configurable document text trim limits.

The gap is around media and design formats. RenameClick's docs do not list camera RAW formats or design files like Sketch/Figma/PSD, and they explicitly call video files, archives, and executables unsupported. If those formats matter, Zush is safer.

## Local AI is parity; naming control is not

Both products can run local models, so offline processing is not a unique advantage. Zush connects to LM Studio or Ollama; RenameClick bundles Qwen3-VL after a multi-GB download and also supports LM Studio and Ollama. The more important difference is the rest of the workflow: Zush documents ready-made Templates, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, richer localization and metadata controls, and persistent Rename History.

Zush wins if you care more about cloud-provider choice and high-volume BYOK. Zush supports Gemini, OpenRouter, OpenAI, and Claude through BYOK, with keys stored locally, and offers LM Studio and Ollama when local processing is the priority. OpenRouter and Claude are notable Zush-side options; Alibaba Cloud Qwen remains a RenameClick-side option.

The practical trade-off is simple: choose Zush for naming depth, recovery detail, and mixed-file breadth. Consider RenameClick only when AI Search or folder movement outweighs its missing RAW, design, iWork, and video coverage.

## Automation and organization

Both tools can watch folders, but they emphasize different outcomes.

Zush folder monitoring is built around automatic AI renaming with reusable templates and Naming Blocks. It is a good fit for Downloads cleanup, screenshots, recurring client folders, and predictable naming conventions where new files should get searchable names as they arrive.

RenameClick Auto Flow is more of a rule-row system. Each row can have its own source folder, destination folder, naming format, custom instructions, language, sorting preset, recursive setting, and Auto Apply setting. It can run multiple rows at the same time, wait for files to become stable before processing, avoid looped processing inside destination folders, and pause when free-plan credits run out.

If your automation goal is "rename files consistently", Zush is the stronger documented system. Consider RenameClick only when the same action must also move files; its narrower format coverage remains the cost of that choice.

## Search, metadata, and retrieval

Zush helps retrieval by generating meaningful filenames, adding Finder tags, and writing Spotlight-friendly metadata. That fits Mac users who already search through Finder/Spotlight and want filenames to carry the most important context.

RenameClick adds a separate AI Search workspace. It lets you search by content meaning, not just filename keywords. For example, a search for invoices can match PDF scans even if the original filename is `scan_001.pdf`. AI Search is read-only for rename/move purposes, but it can reveal files, exclude results, or move items to trash.

For discovery inside chaotic folders, RenameClick's AI Search is a meaningful differentiator. For turning files into long-term searchable assets across the OS, Zush's filename + tag + metadata workflow is the better everyday layer.

## Pick by use case

**Pick Zush if:**

- You need the broadest format coverage, especially RAW photos, design files, videos, iWork, and subtitles
- You want ready-made Templates, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, formatting, localization, metadata, and full Rename History
- You want BYOK with Gemini, OpenRouter, OpenAI, or Claude available on every plan
- You need 60+ output languages
- You want templates and 145+ Naming Blocks for repeatable filename structures
- You want one rename workflow across Mac and Windows without thinking about local model hardware first

**Consider RenameClick only if:**

- You need AI Search by file content
- You want to rename and move files into folders in the same automation
- You rely on EXIF-heavy photo naming patterns
- You specifically need Alibaba Cloud Qwen or RenameClick's folder-routing workflow
- You accept no documented RAW, design, iWork, or video support and the local model's multi-GB hardware footprint

Before buying, copy the same ten representative files into a test folder and run them through both free workflows. Compare name quality, preview clarity, processing time, unsupported formats, and the ability to reverse the complete batch.

## Migration tip

If you are coming from RenameClick to Zush, test the files that RenameClick does not publicly cover first: RAW photos, design files, videos, and iWork documents. If those are the reason you are switching, the difference will be obvious quickly. If you already use a cloud provider key, add it in Zush BYOK and keep provider costs separate from the desktop app.

We do not recommend moving from Zush to RenameClick for the general workflow: you would give up documented RAW, design, iWork, and video coverage while taking on a multi-GB local model and higher published hardware guidance. Consider that move only when AI Search or rename-plus-folder-routing is mandatory and has been verified on copied representative files.

## FAQ

### Is RenameClick cheaper than Zush?

It depends on the plan and local VAT. Zush is $10/month or $48 one-time. RenameClick is $8+ VAT/month or $48+ VAT lifetime. The monthly totals are close after VAT in many markets, while Zush has the lower lifetime checkout total because both start at $48 and RenameClick adds VAT.

### Does RenameClick work offline?

Yes. RenameClick is local-first. After the local model is downloaded, the built-in local provider can run without internet, without an API key, and without API costs. Cloud providers require internet and your own provider key.

### Does Zush work offline?

Yes. Zush supports LM Studio and Ollama for users who want to choose and manage compatible local models. Both local routes are available on Mac and Windows.

### Does RenameClick support RAW photos?

RenameClick's public supported-file list does not include camera RAW formats like CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, DNG, ORF, RAF, RW2, PEF, SRW, or SR2. Zush explicitly supports those RAW formats.

### Does RenameClick support video or audio files?

Audio yes, video no. Its docs now list MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, and AAC with local speech transcription, while video files (`.mp4`, `.mov`, `.mkv`, `.webm`), other audio codecs, archives, and executables are explicitly unsupported. Zush supports 16 video formats alongside its audio list.

### Which has better folder automation?

It depends on the goal. Zush is better for automatic naming with templates and Naming Blocks. RenameClick is better for folder routing because Auto Flow can rename, categorize, and move files into destination folders.
