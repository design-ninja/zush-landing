Zush is a desktop AI file renamer for mixed folders. Its strongest advantage is the naming system: built-in Templates for common workflows, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, prompts, field order, case, separators, date formats, language, Finder tags, Spotlight metadata, and per-folder Template assignments. Rename History stores original and new names, folder, and timestamp and can revert an applied batch. Its 100+ formats span images, RAW, design, documents, iWork, subtitles, video, and audio.

The important part in this comparison is that Zush does not force a file-type silo. A folder with `IMG_4821.HEIC`, `_DSC0019.NEF`, `checkout-flow.fig`, `voice-memo.m4a`, `client-brief.pages`, `recording.mov`, and `invoice.pdf` can stay in one workflow.

Pricing is simple: $10/month or $48 one-time for PRO. Both paid plans remove the rename limit across all four modes: Zush Cloud AI, BYOK, LM Studio, and Ollama. Templates, Naming Blocks, and custom prompts are included free.

One Zush license covers up to 3 devices on Mac and Windows.

You can [export Templates](/docs/template-import-export) with Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, and folder rules, then import them on another computer. Choose local destinations, folder permissions, and AI connections again there; this transfers the workflow settings, not your files.

RenameClick is a local-first desktop app for macOS and Windows. After the local model download, its built-in provider can process supported files on the device without an API key. It also connects to LM Studio and Ollama. RenameClick supports arbitrary OpenAI-compatible APIs, including OpenRouter, Groq, Mistral, DeepSeek, and Langdock, with multiple saved API configurations. Version 3.0.0 on October 13 widened everyday sorting to 12 folders and taught Match Folders to use nested folders and folders organized by year. Version 3.1.0 on October 15 added a side panel for creating and editing naming formats, instructions, and folder sets, with a preview on a real file before saving.

RenameClick is not only a renamer. It is closer to a file organizer: it can rename files, categorize them, move them into folders, run Auto Flow watch rules, search by content with AI Search, and build names from EXIF/date placeholders. Its supported-files documentation now covers three categories: common images (JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, BMP, SVG, HEIC, HEIF, TIFF), a long document list (PDF, DOC/DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, TXT, MD, CSV, RTF, ODT, JSON, XML, YAML, HTML, logs, SQL, SRT/VTT, ICS/VCF), and audio (MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, AAC) with local speech transcription. Video files, other audio codecs, archives, and executables are explicitly listed as unsupported.

## Format support

### Zush is broader for real mixed folders

Zush supports RAW photo formats, design files, audio, videos, iWork files, Office files, subtitles, and data/text formats. That is the practical advantage if your folders are messy because they are real: screenshots beside phone photos, camera RAWs beside PDFs, Figma/Sketch/PSD files beside Keynote/Pages documents, and video files beside meeting notes. Audio is no longer part of that gap — RenameClick handles common audio formats now — but RAW, design files, iWork, and video still are. For media files Zush uses several kinds of evidence at once: sampled frames, readable subtitles including subtitle tracks inside MKV files, a speech excerpt, and track metadata. It can also identify music when there is a reliable catalog match. See [audio and video analysis](/docs/audio-video).

### RenameClick covers common images, a wide document list, and now audio

RenameClick covers common image and document workflows well — JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, BMP, SVG, HEIC, HEIF, TIFF, plus PDF, DOC/DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, TXT, Markdown, CSV, RTF, ODT, JSON, XML, YAML, HTML, logs, SQL, subtitles, and calendar/contact files — and it has added audio (MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, AAC) with local speech transcription. It also has useful document processing details: text extraction for text PDFs and Office documents, vision fallback for scanned PDFs, and configurable document text trim limits.

The gap is around media and design formats. RenameClick's docs do not list camera RAW formats or design files like Sketch/Figma/PSD, and they explicitly call video files, archives, and executables unsupported. If those formats matter, Zush is safer.

## Local AI is parity; naming control is not

Both products can run local models, so offline processing is not a unique advantage. Zush connects to LM Studio or Ollama; RenameClick bundles Qwen3-VL after a multi-GB download and also supports LM Studio and Ollama. The more important difference is the rest of the workflow: Zush documents ready-made Templates, 145+ Naming Blocks, Custom AI Blocks, richer localization and metadata controls, and persistent Rename History.

OpenRouter access is not exclusive to Zush, and provider count does not establish a winner. RenameClick's direct builds accept custom OpenAI-compatible endpoints. Zush combines included managed cloud AI with BYOK for Gemini, OpenRouter, OpenAI, and Claude, plus LM Studio and Ollama. Zush's useful distinction for recurring mixed folders is the combination of included cloud processing, structured filename Templates, documented formats, and Rename History. Compare the exact model, image support, and app distribution you need; external API usage is billed by the provider in either app.

The practical trade-off is simple: choose Zush for naming depth, recovery detail, mixed-file breadth, and prompt-based folder sorting. Consider RenameClick only when its AI Search workspace outweighs the missing RAW, design, iWork, and video coverage.

## Automation and organization

Zush supports nested folder rules and sorting already renamed files without another rename credit on Mac and Windows. This is separate from Monitor processing new arrivals: changing a monitored Template does not automatically reorganize earlier files. See the [folder sorting guide](/docs/folder-sorting), [Mac release notes](/changelog), and [Windows release notes](/changelog/windows).

Both tools can watch folders, but they emphasize different outcomes.

Zush folder monitoring is built around automatic AI renaming with reusable templates and Naming Blocks. A sorting-enabled Template also moves each new file into a folder chosen by your plain-language Folder rules or Auto, and Monitor keeps reusing the folders it created. It fits Downloads cleanup, scanner folders, screenshots, and recurring client folders.

RenameClick Auto Flow is more of a rule-row system. Each row can have its own source folder, destination folder, naming format, custom instructions, language, sorting preset, recursive setting, and Auto Apply setting. It can run multiple rows at the same time, wait for files to become stable before processing, avoid looped processing inside destination folders, and pause when free-plan credits run out.

If your automation goal is to rename files consistently and file them by rules you describe in your own words, Zush is the stronger documented system. Consider RenameClick when picking from folder sets, or matching against folders you already keep, is all you need; its narrower format coverage remains the cost of that choice.

## Search, metadata, and retrieval

Zush helps retrieval by generating meaningful filenames, adding Finder tags, and writing Spotlight-friendly metadata. That fits Mac users who already search through Finder/Spotlight and want filenames to carry the most important context.

RenameClick adds a separate AI Search workspace. It lets you search by content meaning, not just filename keywords. For example, a search for invoices can match PDF scans even if the original filename is `scan_001.pdf`. Its current AI Search workflow can suggest names and apply rename or move actions from results. Indexing and searching do not consume apply credits; applied changes follow the rename allowance.

For discovery inside chaotic folders, RenameClick's AI Search is a meaningful differentiator. For turning files into long-term searchable assets across the OS, Zush's filename + tag + metadata workflow is the better everyday layer.

- You need AI Search by file content

Before buying, copy the same ten representative files into a test folder and run them through both free workflows. Compare name quality, preview clarity, processing time, unsupported formats, and the ability to reverse the complete batch.

## FAQ

### When should I choose Zush over RenameClick?

Choose Zush for a Mac or Windows folder containing RAW photos, design files, iWork documents, audio, and video that needs a reusable naming convention. Templates, 145+ Naming Blocks, preview, and Rename History support that workflow, and PRO includes managed cloud AI. RenameClick has a bundled local model and AI Search; its published format list still excludes video and does not list RAW, design, or iWork support.

### Does RenameClick support OpenRouter?

Yes. RenameClick supports OpenRouter and other OpenAI-compatible APIs in direct Mac and Windows builds but not the Mac App Store build. API usage is billed separately by the connected provider. Zush also supports OpenRouter through BYOK on every plan, so OpenRouter access alone is not a reason to switch.

### Is RenameClick cheaper than Zush?

Zush is $10/month or $48 lifetime. RenameClick is $8 plus VAT/month or $36 plus VAT lifetime.

### Does RenameClick work offline?

Yes. RenameClick is local-first. After the local model is downloaded, the built-in local provider can run without internet, without an API key, and without API costs. Cloud providers require internet and your own provider key.

### Does Zush work offline?

Yes. Zush supports LM Studio and Ollama for users who want to choose and manage compatible local models. Both local routes are available on Mac and Windows.

### Does RenameClick support RAW photos?

RenameClick's public supported-file list does not include camera RAW formats like CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, DNG, ORF, RAF, RW2, PEF, SRW, or SR2. Zush explicitly supports those RAW formats.

### Does RenameClick support video or audio files?

Audio yes, video no. Its docs now list MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, and AAC with local speech transcription, while video files (`.mp4`, `.mov`, `.mkv`, `.webm`), other audio codecs, archives, and executables are explicitly unsupported. Zush supports video on Mac and Windows, including MP4, MOV, MKV, AVI, and WMV. See the [supported format list](/docs/supported-file-types).

### Which has better folder automation?

Zush, for most folders. Both apps can rename new files and move them into folders. Zush sorts by plain-language Folder rules or Auto, reuses existing folders, and names files with Templates and Naming Blocks. RenameClick's Auto Flow sorts into folder sets or existing subfolders up to three levels deep, offers 12 folder options per file by default and up to 50, and can copy instead of move.
